# Prostheti Innovations Campaign - Plot Outline

## Overview
A 7-chapter campaign centered around the Wells Family and their company, **Prostheti Innovations** - a manufacturer and seller of prosthetics and augments. Founded by Clyde Wells himself after he left a Wing-adjacent augment manufacturer, the company has grown from a rented garage into a legitimate Backstreets operation. The campaign is set in the **outer reaches of District 20 (T Corp.)**, far enough from the District's center that the sepia color drain and extreme time differences are less pronounced, but still embedded in the District's culture of technology and innovation.

## Characters

### Main Characters

#### Clyde Wells
- **Age:** 46
- **Role:** CEO and founder of Prostheti Innovations
- **Description:** Blonde hair slicked back, facial scarring or augmentation visible on the right side of his face. Wears a dark navy coat with a fur-lined brown collar over a light blue shirt. Often seen smoking. Has a hardened, weathered appearance that speaks to a man who has been through more than his years should suggest.
- **Personality:** Cautious, pragmatic, and deeply scarred by personal loss. Once warm and community-oriented, he has become emotionally guarded and strategically minded - not out of callousness, but out of hard-won experience that being visible and connected makes you a target.
- **Motivations:** Genuinely wants to improve the lives of people in his district. He believes the most effective and long-term solution is to grow Prostheti Innovations' influence and economic dominance so the company can enact real systemic change from a position of power and safety. He wants Penny to continue this mission as the next leader - not out of ego or legacy, but because he truly believes this is the right path.
- **Backstory:** Before founding Prostheti Innovations, Clyde worked quality control at a Wing-adjacent augment manufacturer - a big name with Nest headquarters and corporate everything. Good pay, stable work, the kind of job people in the Backstreets would kill for. But he watched them cut corners on Backstreets-grade product: same brand name, different internals. Components that would fail in months - joint servos that locked up, nerve interfaces that degraded - sold to people who had spent their savings on a new arm and couldn't afford a replacement when it broke. He filed reports, flagged components, documented everything. Nothing changed. The margins on cheap product were too good. Eventually he took the blueprints for their best-selling prosthetic line and walked out. The blueprints weren't patented Singularity tech - just good engineering they'd never bothered to protect - so sending Fixers after a quality control nobody over non-patented schematics wasn't worth the ahn. He was lucky. He built the first batch of augments by hand in a rented garage in the Backstreets, sold them to a Fixer who liked the craftsmanship, and word of mouth did the rest. That garage became Prostheti Innovations.

  Clyde and his wife originally took a hands-on, community-connected approach to running the company. His wife - a former **Section 2 Cinq Association Fixer** from the South branch - brought her reputation, her Fixer network, and her philosophy of direct engagement into the company. They were local, visible, and personally invested in the people around their district - offering affordable prosthetics, doing community outreach, even providing free maintenance to Backstreets residents who couldn't afford repairs. Clyde felt safe about this approach because his wife was a combat-tested Association professional. If trouble came, she could handle it.

  As Prostheti Innovations grew in influence and began gaining recognition in the Inventor system, they became targets. The **Insurgence Clan** warned them to back off, but Clyde dismissed the threat - partly out of principle, partly because he had a Section 2 Cinq fixer at his side. This proved catastrophic. The Clan did not challenge them to a fair fight. They exploited the **Night in the Backstreets** and struck from the shadows, **assassinating his wife right in front of him**. All her skill, her rank, her years of Cinq combat experience - none of it mattered against an enemy that refused to fight fair.

  After this devastating loss, Clyde pulled back entirely from direct community engagement. The lesson he took was specific: skill doesn't protect you. Visibility is what kills you. He became distant and focused solely on what he believes is the safest, most effective path forward: growing the company's power through the patent system, Inventor certifications, and economic influence from behind closed doors. He replaced Cinq-style directness with Zwei-style protection - hiring shields rather than trusting swords.
- **Notable Details:** His current philosophy is a direct reaction to trauma. He isn't wrong that direct engagement is dangerous - he has the scars to prove it (his facial scarring may be from the same attack that killed his wife). This makes his conflict with Penny especially tragic: she wants to do exactly what he and his wife once did, and he knows firsthand where that road leads. Wears an expensive T Corp. timepiece - one of the few luxuries he allows himself, and a quiet marker of the company's financial standing.
- **Why Clyde Stayed Silent:** Clyde already knew who Hector was before any letters arrived. Penny and Hector grew up together in the outer Backstreets - they were childhood friends, and Clyde was aware of Hector's existence the same way any parent knows their child's friends. When Prostheti Innovations was still community-connected, when Clyde and his wife were out in the streets doing outreach, Penny and Hector were kids playing in the same neighborhoods. Clyde had no reason to view Hector as a threat. He was just a Backstreets boy his daughter had grown up with - unremarkable, familiar, part of the background of their old life.

  This prior familiarity is what made the letters feel manageable rather than alarming. When Clyde intercepted the first letters and saw Hector's name, he didn't see a stranger contacting his daughter - he saw a childhood friend she was reconnecting with. The return address listed a competitor company, which raised his corporate instincts, but the person behind the letters was someone he vaguely remembered from before the assassination. Someone harmless. This is why Clyde's response was surveillance rather than intervention: the situation didn't feel dangerous enough to warrant confrontation, just suspicious enough to warrant watching.

  But what kept Clyde silent was not just corporate caution - it was something far more personal. When he read the letters and learned that Penny was training to become a Fixer, he did not see a teenager picking up a hobby. He saw his wife. The same idealism. The same conviction that the right thing to do was show up in person, be visible, be present, put yourself in danger for people who needed help. Penny's letters read like echoes of conversations Clyde had with his wife years ago - the same arguments about how the district needed direct action, not corporate strategy. The same belief that being there *mattered* more than being safe.

  And Clyde knew how that story ended. His wife had believed all the same things Penny believed. She had been community-connected, hands-on, present in the Backstreets. She confronted the Insurgence Clan's warnings head-on. She refused to back down. And they killed her for it - right in front of him.

  This is what made the conversation impossible. Confronting Penny about the training didn't just mean discussing her future - it meant confronting *why* her Fixer dream terrified him so deeply. It meant telling her the truth about her mother: *"She died because she believed what you believe. She wanted to help people directly. She was brave and visible and engaged - and the world punished her for it."* Clyde had never had that conversation honestly with Penny. He had never been able to say those words out loud, because saying them meant admitting two things he could not face: that he blamed himself for not stopping his wife, and that he saw the same road opening up under his daughter's feet.

  If he confronted Penny and she listened - really listened - she might crumble under the weight of knowing her dream was her mother's death sentence. And if she didn't listen? If she looked him in the eye and said she was going to do it anyway? Then Clyde would have to watch his daughter walk the same path that killed her mother, fully informed, fully warned, and be completely powerless to stop it. Either outcome was unbearable.

  So he watched. Surveillance gave him the feeling of control without the risk of the conversation. He could tell himself he was managing the situation - allowing the training because combat skills were useful for a future CEO, keeping tabs on Hector because the competitor connection was suspicious, staying ready to intervene if things escalated. He convinced himself Penny would grow out of it. She was a teenager; surely, after enough time, she'd see the Fixer path for what it was. He framed Hector's training as something he was *permitting* rather than something happening behind his back - a mental trick that let him feel in control of a situation he was choosing not to address.

  Every day he didn't have the conversation, he could tell himself it wasn't necessary yet. For a year, "not yet" was enough.

  This is exactly what Hector was counting on.

#### Vivian Wells
- **Age:** 34 (at time of death; would be 45 if alive)
- **Status:** Deceased (assassinated)
- **Role:** Former co-leader of Prostheti Innovations alongside Clyde; ex-Cinq Association Fixer
- **Description:** TODO - awaiting further details
- **Backstory:** Born in the Backstreets of District 20, she grew up surrounded by factories, sepia-tinted streets, and the grind of time poverty that defines life in T Corp.'s outer reaches. She got her Hana license the day she turned 20 and entered the Fixer world through combat - eventually joining the **Cinq Association's South branch**, where duels are declared by throwing a glove and fought under strict rules of etiquette and honor.

  She was good. Over the years she climbed to **Section 2** of the South branch - one tier below the Section Directors, operating at roughly Grade 3 in the broader Fixer system. In a profession where most people die young or burn out before they reach Section 4, reaching Section 2 meant something. She had a reputation in the southern quarter: disciplined, precise, and known for ending duels cleanly. Cinq Fixers fight face-to-face with declared intent - no ambushes, no tricks, no hidden blades. She carried that philosophy into everything she did.

  She met Clyde through the prosthetics industry. Fixers virtually require augmentation to survive their work, and Prostheti Innovations was a growing manufacturer in District 20's competitive market. What started as a business relationship - a Cinq fixer who needed reliable maintenance on her combat augments, a company that needed someone credible to vouch for their products in the Fixer market - became personal. They married, and she stepped back from active Association work to co-lead the company alongside Clyde.

  But she didn't leave her influence behind. A Section 2 Cinq fixer endorsing your prosthetics carries enormous weight in a world where every Fixer needs augments to survive. She leveraged her reputation and her network to promote Prostheti Innovations directly - not just through patent filings and Expo submissions, but through the Fixer community itself. She personally fitted augments for Fixers she knew. She offered free maintenance to Backstreets residents who couldn't afford repairs on their cheap, glitching prosthetics. She used Cinq's occasional joint conferences and events with other Associations to showcase the company's work to a wider audience. Under her influence, Prostheti Innovations wasn't just a manufacturer - it was a presence in the district. People knew the company because they knew *her*.

  This community-connected approach was genuine. She believed - as Clyde once did - that a company with real resources had an obligation to use them directly. District 20's outer Backstreets were full of factory workers with degrading augments they couldn't afford to fix, children carrying machine parts, and people living on the wrong side of T Corp.'s time inequality. She showed up for those people. Not from behind a corporate desk, but in person - the same directness that defined her Cinq career.

  Her Cinq background also made Clyde feel safe about this approach. He wasn't blind to the risks of being visible in the Backstreets, but his wife was a combat-tested Association professional who had spent years fighting face-to-face at the highest levels. If trouble came to their door, she could handle it. She had handled worse. This confidence is part of why Clyde dismissed the Insurgence Clan's warnings when they told him to back off - he had a Section 2 Cinq fixer watching his family's back. What could a Backstreets faction do that she couldn't counter?

  The answer was: not fight fair. The Insurgence Clan did not throw a glove. They did not declare intent. They did not challenge her to a duel under Cinq rules and etiquette. They exploited the **Night in the Backstreets** - the 81-minute window when anything goes and no one is held responsible - and struck from the shadows using their human operatives. An assassination during the Night is the antithesis of everything Cinq represents: no honor, no rules, no chance to respond. All her skill, her rank, her years of Section 2 combat experience - none of it mattered against an attack she never saw coming. She was killed right in front of Clyde.

  This is the specific lesson Clyde took from her death, and it is the foundation of everything he became afterward. It wasn't just that direct engagement was dangerous - it was that *skill doesn't protect you*. His wife was one of the best close-combat fighters in the southern quarter, and the Clan killed her like she was nothing. If a Section 2 Cinq fixer couldn't survive being visible and community-connected, then visibility itself was the problem. You don't fight threats head-on - you manage them from a distance, where they can't reach you. You build walls of wealth and corporate influence. You hire other professionals (the Zwei, not the Cinq - protection, not dueling) to handle danger on your behalf. You never, ever put yourself where the enemy can see you.

  This is also what makes Penny's Fixer dream so specifically terrifying to Clyde. Penny isn't just following an abstract ideal - she is walking her mother's exact path. The same community-minded directness. The same belief that showing up matters more than staying safe. The same conviction that being a Fixer means something. Clyde watched that path kill the most capable fighter he ever knew. Now his daughter wants to walk it, and she doesn't even have a Section 2 Cinq rank to fall back on - she has a year of secret training with a childhood friend. When Clyde looks at Penny's Fixer ambitions, he doesn't see hope. He sees his wife's funeral.

- **Narrative Role:** Her death is the pivotal event that shaped Clyde into who he is today and created the philosophical divide between him and Penny. Her Cinq background adds a critical dimension: Clyde's retreat into corporate isolation wasn't cowardice or overcaution. He watched the most skilled fighter he knew get killed by an enemy that refused to fight fair. His conclusion - that visibility is death regardless of how strong you are - is not irrational. It's the lesson the City taught him. Penny's desire to become a Fixer is not just idealism repeating itself; it is Penny unknowingly choosing to become her mother, and Clyde knowing exactly how that story ends.

#### Penny Wells
- **Age:** 19
- **Role:** Daughter of Clyde Wells, would-be 2nd generation leader of Prostheti Innovations
- **Description:** TODO - awaiting further details
- **Personality:** Action-oriented, community-minded, and more clear-eyed than people give her credit for. She feels a strong pull toward directly helping the people of her district rather than leading from behind a corporate desk.

  Follows famous Fixers and Color Fixers closely - she knows their names, their Offices, their exploits - but she is not naive about what the Fixer system actually is. She understands that no Association operates out of heroism. The Zwei protect clients because they're paid to, not out of altruism. The Shi will assassinate anyone with equal impartiality - target's crimes are irrelevant. The Liu burn through battlefields for commission, not cause. Even the Seven, who investigate and solve cases, do it as contracted work - and charge for "post-contract management" when it's time to punish the people responsible. Every Association is a business, every Fixer is a mercenary, and the entire system exists to make money and accumulate power within the City. Penny knows all of this.
- **Motivations:** She still wants to become a Fixer - and she can articulate why, even knowing the system's ugliness:
  - **Fixers actually show up.** When something goes wrong in the Backstreets, Wings don't care, the Head's reach doesn't extend there, and corporate influence takes years to trickle down. But a Fixer shows up. Even if they're doing it for Ahn, the result is someone getting helped *today*. Her father's strategy might improve the district in a generation. The people outside the factory walls need someone now.
  - **Individual choice still exists within the system.** Associations are mercenary, but individual Fixers and Offices choose which jobs to take. An independent Office can be selective - take protection work over assassination contracts, prioritize Backstreets clients over Nest vanity jobs. The system is rotten, but a person inside it can still choose to do good with the tools it gives them.
  - **She's seen corporate influence from the inside, and it hasn't changed anything yet.** Prostheti Innovations has been growing for years under her father's strategy. The outer Backstreets are still full of children carrying machine parts, people with glitching cheap augmentations they can't afford to fix, and factory workers who will never see the Nest. The company is doing well. The district is not. She doesn't believe another decade of patent filings and Expo awards will change that.
  - **She can combine her family's expertise with direct action.** She has grown up around prosthetics and augmentation - she understands the technology intimately. A Fixer with genuine prosthetics knowledge could maintain their own augmentations, help injured civilians, and operate without being dependent on expensive Workshop contracts. Her background gives her an edge most Fixer hopefuls don't have.
  - She is fully aware that being a Fixer is far more dangerous than being the next CEO. Fixers die constantly - it's so common the City barely notices. Augmentation is virtually required just to survive the work, and even then the life expectancy is brutal. She knows this is not the safe path. She is choosing it anyway, because safety is exactly what her father chose after her mother died, and from where Penny stands, all that safety bought was a locked door between the company and the people it was supposed to help.
  - Fixer licenses from the Hana Association require a minimum age of 20 and physical fitness - Penny is 19, exactly one year away from eligibility, which gives her secret training an urgent ticking clock.
- **Notable Details:** Has been secretly training in combat with her childhood friend Hector (they grew up together in the outer Backstreets), preparing herself so she can apply for her Fixer license the moment she turns 20. Despite Hector working for a direct competitor to Prostheti Innovations, she doesn't see this as a problem. Communicates with Hector by letter (District 20 lacks advanced communication systems - physical mail only).

### Supporting Characters

#### Hector
- **Age:** 22
- **Role:** Penny's genuine childhood friend turned Insurgence Clan operative; runs the Clan's augment shop in District 20. Known within the Clan as the "Major Controller."
- **Description (cover identity):** TODO - his everyday appearance as Penny's friend and training partner. Presented himself as an employee at a competitor prosthetics company.
- **Description (true identity):** Dark hooded cloak over glowing orange hexagonal armor plating. Face completely hidden beneath the hood. Bladed weapons on his back. The armor doesn't match any known manufacturer or Workshop - it's Insurgence Clan technology of unclear origin. Menacing and high-tech in a way that nothing in the Backstreets should be.
- **Personality:** Patient, methodical, and tactically sharp. The friendship with Penny was real - they grew up together in the outer Backstreets of District 20. But years of working within the Insurgence Clan changed him, and by the time the Clan assigned him to deliver her, he was willing to do it. During the boss fight he is controlled and tactical, not raging.
- **Motivations:** Hector grew up in the same outer Backstreets as Penny, but without a CEO father or a company to fall back on. Years before the Wells operation ever began, Hector hit a wall - the kind of desperate situation (debt, a threat to someone he cared about, or simply the slow crush of Backstreets poverty) that the City offers no way out of. The Insurgence Clan gave him a lifeline when no one else would: augments, money, a place. He became one of their operatives - not a fresh recruit, but someone who had been working within their network for years, rising through the ranks, eventually earning enough trust to run the Clan's unregistered augment shop in District 20. The Clan was his life. It was the only structure that had ever offered him anything.

  When the Clan's higher-ups identified Penny Wells as a target and discovered that Hector - their own operative - had a genuine childhood connection to her, they assigned him the operation. Hector's debt to the Clan was deep enough that refusing was never a real option. They had pulled him out of nothing, given him everything he had, and now they were calling it in. But the debt alone didn't make him go through with it - what made it possible was that he genuinely believed he was saving her. He knows what happens to idealistic kids in the Backstreets. He's watched people die trying to be Fixers. Penny was 19, one year from applying for a Hana license, training in secret to throw herself into one of the most lethal professions in the City - and she thought she was ready. Hector didn't. He convinced himself - or was convinced - that wherever the Clan was taking her, she'd at least be alive. Becoming a Fixer in the outer Backstreets is a death sentence. The Clan was offering something else.

  This is a twisted mirror of Clyde's own logic: both men betrayed Penny's autonomy because they decided they knew better than she did about what was safe. The debt forced his hand; the belief that he was protecting her let him live with it.
- **Notable Details:**
  - The friendship with Penny was **genuine** - they grew up together in the outer Backstreets. This makes his betrayal far more personal than a cold infiltration would be. He knew exactly who he was handing over
  - His professional identity (employment at the competitor company, letter return addresses) was **designed to be disposable** - it dissolved entirely the moment the operation was complete, leaving Clyde with nothing to trace
  - Runs the Insurgence Clan's unregistered augment shop - selling augments with no TAA registration, undercutting legitimate manufacturers like Prostheti Innovations
  - Has a **kill switch** built into the augments of his guards, consistent with the Clan's pattern of controlling people through their hardware
  - After being defeated and interrogated, he is sent to Clyde
- **The Long Game - Hector's Manipulation of the Wells Family:** Hector's operation was not just about delivering Penny to the Insurgence Clan - it was about systematically destroying the relationship between father and daughter so that when the moment came, Penny would have no one to turn to except him. Hector had been a Clan operative for years before the Wells family ever became a target. When the Clan's higher-ups identified Penny and discovered Hector's childhood connection to her, they assigned him the operation roughly two years before Chapter 1. He spent the first year building a disposable cover identity and studying the Wells family's dynamics from a distance. The active phase of the operation - reconnecting with Penny, starting the letters, beginning the combat training - started about **one year** before Chapter 1, when Penny was 18 and beginning to seriously think about her Fixer ambitions. The manipulation unfolded in deliberate stages:

  1. **Make the letters visible.** Hector knew Clyde - and more importantly, Clyde already knew Hector. They weren't strangers. Penny and Hector had been childhood friends; Clyde remembered him from the old days when the company was still community-connected. Hector exploited this familiarity deliberately. When he began writing letters to Penny, he didn't hide them well - but he also didn't need to appear threatening. He used a return address tied to a competitor company, which was enough to trigger Clyde's corporate paranoia, but the name on the letters was a childhood friend, not an unknown contact. This combination - familiar name, suspicious employer - was calibrated to make Clyde start reading without escalating to direct action. A stranger's letters might have prompted Clyde to confront Penny or investigate Hector immediately. A childhood friend's letters from a competitor? That warranted *watching*. Hector made sure Clyde would discover the correspondence on his own and draw his own conclusions. He left letters where Clyde's household staff or security would find them. A father who already monitored everything would see letters from a boy at a rival company and immediately start reading them - but because the boy was someone he vaguely remembered and considered harmless, he would read quietly rather than act.

  2. **Let Clyde's trauma paralyze him.** This was the cruelest part of Hector's plan, and the part that required the deepest understanding of who Clyde was. Hector was not merely counting on Clyde to stay silent out of habit - he was counting on the specific trauma that made Clyde incapable of having this conversation.

     When Clyde read the letters and learned that Penny was training to become a Fixer, he did not see a teenager with a hobby. He saw his wife. The same idealism - wanting to help people directly, refusing to accept that safety required distance. The same willingness to be visible, to be present, to put herself in harm's way for others. Penny's Fixer dream was her mother's philosophy reborn, and Clyde knew exactly where that philosophy ended: with blood on the floor and a woman who would never come home.

     This connection between Penny's training and his wife's death is what made the topic impossible for Clyde to approach. Confronting Penny about the training meant confronting *why* it terrified him - which meant talking about her mother's assassination. Clyde had never had that conversation honestly. He had never sat Penny down and said: *"Your mother died because she believed what you believe. She wanted to help people directly. She was visible, she was engaged, she was brave - and the Insurgence Clan killed her for it."* He couldn't say it. Partly because he blamed himself for not stopping her. Partly because he was afraid that if he told Penny the truth, she would either crumble under the weight of it or - worse - look him in the eye and say she was going to do it anyway. And then he would have to watch his daughter choose the same road that killed her mother, fully informed, and be powerless to stop it.

     So instead of talking, Clyde did what his trauma trained him to do: he watched. He intercepted. He monitored. Surveillance gave him the feeling of control without the risk of the conversation. He could tell himself he was managing the situation - allowing the training because combat skills were useful, keeping tabs on Hector because the competitor connection was suspicious, staying ready to intervene if things escalated. As long as he was watching, he didn't have to speak. As long as he didn't speak, he didn't have to face what Penny's dream actually meant to him.

     Hector understood all of this. He understood that Clyde's silence was not laziness or indifference - it was a man frozen between his need to protect his daughter and his inability to confront the memory of his wife's death. Every letter Hector sent was a bet that Clyde would keep watching and keep quiet. For a year, Clyde never once proved him wrong.

  3. **Become the person Clyde refuses to be.** While Clyde watched in silence, Hector talked. He trained with Penny, encouraged her dreams, listened to her frustrations about her father, and treated her Fixer ambitions as something worth pursuing rather than a phase to be tolerated. Penny had a father who knew everything about her life and said nothing, and a friend who showed up, engaged directly, and made her feel heard. Over the course of a year, the contrast became unbearable. Hector didn't need to turn Penny against Clyde - Clyde's silence did that on its own. Hector just had to be present where Clyde was absent.

  4. **Design the test to force the reveal.** The "Fixer test" in Chapter 2 - breaking into the competitor's factory - was never about testing Penny. It was about forcing Clyde's hand. Hector chose a target that was genuinely dangerous, pushed urgency so Penny couldn't hesitate, and then **alerted Clyde the moment they entered the building**. He knew Clyde would deploy the Zwei. He knew the Zwei would extract Penny. And he knew that in the aftermath, Clyde would have to explain *how* he knew where she was - which meant revealing that he had been reading her letters the entire time. The test was a trap for Clyde, not Penny. It was designed to make Clyde expose his own surveillance.

  5. **Let the reveal do the damage.** The moment Clyde admits he has been intercepting Penny's letters, everything Penny believed about her private life collapses. Her secret training, her plans, her personal correspondence with her closest friend - none of it was ever private. Her father knew everything and chose to watch silently rather than speak to her. The betrayal isn't that Clyde intervened to save her life - it's that he had been violating her trust for a year and never said a word. And critically: Hector emerges from the Chapter 2 fallout completely clean. From Penny's perspective, Hector is the one person who has been honest with her. He trained with her openly, encouraged her directly, and never hid anything. Her father watched her in secret for a year. Hector showed up in person. The contrast is devastating - and entirely by design.

  6. **Isolate and capture.** With Penny's trust in Clyde shattered and her trust in Hector absolute, the kidnapping becomes trivial. Penny is emotionally isolated from her father, furious, and desperate for someone who respects her autonomy. When Hector offers her a Nightwatcher's cloak to slip out during the Night in the Backstreets - framed as freedom from her father's cage - she takes it without hesitation. She is walking toward the one person she still trusts. She doesn't know she is walking into a capture.

  The entire operation exploited a single vulnerability: Clyde's inability to talk to his daughter. Hector didn't create the dysfunction in the Wells family - he identified it, widened it over the course of a year, and then used the gap to pull Penny through. Every letter was a wedge. Every training session was a replacement for the relationship Clyde refused to have. And the final test was the hammer that split the family apart so cleanly that Penny ran to Hector for safety from the very man who was trying to protect her.

## World Context

### The City
The City is an oasis in a hostile world - a place where people live in relative peace, free from the dangers of the Outskirts. It maintains detection systems that identify non-humans, and machines are "rejected and destroyed" on sight. Only machines with "reduced consciousness" (simple bots) are tolerated. Even undocumented humans cannot enter without proper papers. The City claims to represent an "age of Humanity" but ignores the humans and machines suffering in the Outskirts. It is protected by forces like Z-Corp Security.

### The Outskirts
A brutal, hostile environment outside the City where hundreds die every week to attacks from beasts and other dangers.

### Fixers
Fixers act as contractors that cover a wide variety of roles in society, working on commission. They often handle lines of work which are considered more dangerous or more specialized than those of average citizens, including direct combat, assassinations, or protection, as well as less violent roles such as trading information, espionage, investigative work or enforcing contracts. To some level, they may act as peacekeepers, being hired to take up jobs more specific to those that hire them. The specialization and accomplishments of Fixers in fields that a majority of citizens do not have allows them to garner great bounds of attention and popularity.

### Chapter 4: Old Debts - "Alleyway Investigation"

If the players approach without aggression, the reading guard questions them - "If you're not buying anything, you better get out of here." If players claim to be augment buyers, the guards let them inside. If players attack, two more guards rush out from inside to help
  - **Inside the Shop (RP Section)**: Inside, two more guards are lounging - one stacking cards in the corner, the other tinkering with their own augments. A secretary sits at a desk. She can set up a meeting with the boss if the players give a reason (buying augments works).

    Players have time to **talk with each of the four guards**, learning their backgrounds. None of them joined the Insurgence Clan out of ideology - they joined because the Backstreets demand you belong to *something*. In the City, it's considered foolish to not be part of a group; individuals are easy targets. The Clan offered each of them what the City wouldn't:
    - **Francis** (The Calm One) - reading a book outside. Thinks the boss underprices augments, which is why his pay is terrible. He's right - the Clan can underprice because they have zero regulatory overhead, no TAA fees or patent costs, but that savings isn't reaching the workers. If players ask about the Clan's other operations, Francis mentions a warehouse run by a guy who **used to be a Seven Fixer** - says the man treats the place more like an information exchange than a holding facility, and that he's heard the guy has let captives walk free more than once because he got what he wanted from them. Francis respects it in a way: "At least someone in this outfit keeps their word." Can be swayed by offering better-paying legitimate work, or enough Ahn up front to make the risk worthwhile
    - **Allen** (The Laidback One) - sleeping on duty outside. Bored out of his mind, never gets to use his augments. Joined because the Clan gave him hardware he couldn't afford otherwise, but guarding a shop wasn't what he signed up for. Can be swayed by offering more active/exciting work
    - **Martha** (The Idealistic One) - stacking cards inside. Praises the Clan and talks about her future in it, but admits she never gets chances to stand out. She tied herself to the Clan for security and belonging - the same reason anyone in the City ties themselves to an organization. Can be swayed by offering her a role where she actually matters
    - **Felix** (The Curious One) - tinkering with augments inside. Fascinated by how the augments work but frustrated the Clan won't share technical details. Only joined because they let him try interesting augments. Can be swayed by offering access to real technical knowledge - TAA-registered documentation, a position at a legitimate manufacturer like Prostheti Innovations, anything that lets him actually *learn* instead of just use
  - By choosing the right dialogue options and offering appropriate gifts, players can convince individual guards to switch sides during the upcoming boss fight. The "gifts" can be concrete City things: Ahn, a job offer at Prostheti Innovations, a reference letter, or access to knowledge the Clan withholds
  - **Meeting the Boss**: After the secretary sets up the meeting, players wait one minute, then receive the key to the boss's room. Inside, the boss sits at his desk flanked by two **Selfless Shield guards** (sleek silver-grey armor with glowing orange-red circular elements). The boss himself wears a dark hooded cloak over glowing orange hexagonal armor plating, face hidden.

    Players who work at Prostheti Innovations - a prosthetics company - would recognize augments from major brands and Workshops. This armor matches *nothing* known. It's Insurgence Clan technology, advanced hardware of unclear origin that shouldn't exist in the Backstreets. He chats with the players casually, even offering a few items for sale. The interaction stays civil until the players either **question him too aggressively** or **try to leave**
  - **The Hector Reveal**: The boss drops his act and reveals that he is **Hector** - Penny's childhood friend and training partner, the person the players trusted in Chapters 1 and 2. A boss fight begins immediately. The four guards from earlier are called in - those that the players swayed through conversation will side with the players; the rest fight alongside Hector.

    Hector fights as a "Major Controller" type, and if any turned guards join the players, Hector can spend a turn activating a **kill switch built into their augments**, eliminating them but giving the players an opening to attack. This is the same pattern from Chapter 3 - the Insurgence Clan builds control mechanisms into the hardware they give their people. The augments are a leash, not a gift
  - **Interrogation**: After Hector is beaten down, the players get a window to question him before sending him to Clyde. He reveals:
    1. The Insurgence Clan had been **documenting the Wells family's personal life** for a long time. Once the Clan's higher-ups learned about Penny, they became fixated on capturing her. Hector was already one of their operatives - he had joined the Clan **years earlier**, pulled out of a desperate situation (debt, a threat to someone he cared about, or simply the grinding poverty of the Backstreets that the City offers no escape from) and had been working within their network ever since. When they discovered that one of their own people had a genuine childhood connection to the girl they wanted, they assigned him the operation. His debt to the Clan was deep enough that refusing wasn't an option
    2. He and Penny genuinely grew up together - the friendship was real, and he doesn't deny it. But **about two years ago**, the Clan assigned him the Wells operation. He spent the first year building a disposable cover identity - the fake job at the competitor company, the return addresses for the letters - and then reconnected with Penny roughly a year before the campaign begins. From that point forward, every interaction with Penny had a second purpose. The training, the letters, the encouragement toward becoming a Fixer - all of it served the operation. His professional identity - the job at the competitor company, the return addresses on the letters - was **designed to be disposable**. The moment the operation was complete, he burned it all and returned to his real position running the Insurgence Clan's augment shop. That's why Clyde hit dead ends everywhere when he tried to find Hector after Penny vanished: there was nothing real to find except a childhood friendship he chose to weaponize
    3. If pressed on **why** - why he would do this to someone he actually knew, actually grew up with - Hector doesn't give the cold mercenary answer. He says he's **seen what happens to people who try to become Fixers in the outer Backstreets**. He's watched people die. Penny was 19, one year from throwing herself into one of the most lethal professions in the City, and she thought weekend training sessions made her ready. He didn't. The Clan told him they wanted her alive - and wherever they were taking her, she'd at least **be alive**. He convinced himself he was saving her from a worse fate.

       This is an uncomfortable parallel to Clyde: both men betrayed Penny's autonomy because they decided they knew better than she did about what was safe. The debt forced his hand; the belief that he was protecting her let him live with it.

       Players can challenge this - point out that the Clan's talk of "curing" people, kill switches, and conversion ideology suggest Penny's fate is far worse than anything the Fixer life would bring. Hector has no answer for that. He doesn't know what happened after the handoff
    4. The letters were part of the operation in a second way: Hector **deliberately made them noticeable enough that Clyde would discover them and begin tracking Penny**. The primary goal was not intelligence-gathering - it was **building the mistrust that would destroy their relationship**. By making Clyde discover the letters on his own, Hector ensured that Clyde would spend a year secretly reading his daughter's most private correspondence, monitoring her movements, and tracking her life - all while saying nothing. When this surveillance was inevitably exposed (which Hector engineered in Chapter 2), the betrayal would shatter Penny's trust in her father completely and leave her with no one to turn to except the one person who had been honest with her: Hector. Clyde thought he was protecting Penny by watching her; in reality, his surveillance was the weapon Hector had built for him, a year-long betrayal waiting to detonate
    5. When the time came, Hector gave Penny a **Nightwatcher's cloak** - gear designed for operatives who move during the **Night in the Backstreets**. The cloak allowed Penny to slip out of the house during the 81-minute window when everyone is locked indoors, no recordings are permitted, and Sweepers consume everything left outside. No witnesses, no evidence, no legal investigation possible. The City's own Taboo made the kidnapping invisible
    6. Once Penny was outside and alone, he captured her and sent her to an **Insurgence Clan warehouse**. He does not know what happened to her after the handoff - only that the higher-ups were very determined to have her. When asked about who runs the warehouse, Hector says it's managed by an **ex-Seven Fixer** who values information above everything else. He describes the man as someone who keeps his word and would rather trade a hostage for useful knowledge than hold them indefinitely - but warns that not everyone at the warehouse agrees with that approach, and there's been growing tension over it

- **Characters Involved:** Hector (as the hidden boss), four Insurgence Clan guards (Francis, Allen, Martha, Felix), secretary (Isabel), two Selfless Shield guards, Clyde Wells (receives Hector at the end), Penny Wells (referenced)

- **Player Objectives:**
  - Gain entry to the Insurgence Clan shop (through diplomacy or force)
  - Talk to the guards and attempt to sway them before the boss fight
  - Meet the boss and discover Hector's true identity
  - Defeat Hector in the boss fight
  - Interrogate Hector and learn what happened to Penny

- **Clyde's Development (Control Weaponized):** This is the most devastating blow to Clyde's worldview. Hector's interrogation reveals that Clyde's surveillance of Penny was *exactly what the Clan wanted* - not as an intelligence source, but as the mechanism that would destroy Penny's trust in her father. The letters were made noticeable on purpose. Clyde's secret monitoring was the betrayal Hector was engineering from the start, a year of hidden spying designed to shatter the relationship between father and daughter when it finally surfaced. The tool he built to protect Penny was turned into the tool that drove her away.

  Players should see genuine self-doubt for the first time. Clyde receiving Hector in custody should not feel triumphant - it should feel hollow. He caught the man who betrayed his daughter, and in the process learned that his own protection was part of the trap. If players talk to Clyde after the interrogation, he is quieter, less certain. He doesn't defend the surveillance anymore. The man who said *"I saved your life because I was paying attention"* in Chapter 2 has learned that paying attention was the problem.

- **Outcome/Consequences:** Hector's betrayal is fully revealed. The players learn that the friendship was real - Hector and Penny genuinely grew up together - but about two years ago the Clan - which Hector had been part of for years - assigned him the Wells operation because of his childhood connection to Penny. After a year of building his cover, he reconnected with her and spent the following year weaponizing everything he had with her to deliver her to the Insurgence Clan. Worse, Clyde's own surveillance was weaponized against him - his secret monitoring of Penny was exactly what Hector wanted, not as an intelligence source, but as the mechanism that destroyed Penny's trust in her father and drove her straight to the one person she still believed in: Hector.

  The kidnapping was executed during the Night in the Backstreets using Insurgence Clan gear specifically designed to exploit the City's blind spot. Hector's unknown-origin augments and the Clan's pattern of building kill switches into their hardware raise questions about where their technology comes from. The unregistered augment shop reveals the Clan has been economically undermining companies like Prostheti Innovations while simultaneously infiltrating the Wells family.

  Clyde now has Hector in custody, but Penny is still missing, last known to be at an Insurgence Clan warehouse. The Clan's fixation on Penny specifically raises questions about what they want with her. The players have a new lead: the warehouse Hector mentioned.

### Chapter 5: Boiling Point - "Hostage Negotiations"
- **Summary:** Following the lead from Chapter 4, the players head to the Insurgence Clan warehouse where Hector said Penny was sent. They attempt to break in but are **captured by the Clan** and restrained on the first floor. While tied down, they are interrogated by **Julio Smith** - the warehouse's Head of Negotiation and an ex-Seven Fixer - who questions them about Clyde and Prostheti Innovations.

  After the questioning, Julio decides to leave the players locked up on the first floor while he plans to move them to the second floor. This is when **Simon Jarvis**, the Head of Security (the "Steamworks Agent"), snaps. He attacks Julio, declaring that letting hostages live is a pattern of weakness - that the players are a major threat who need to be "converted," and that Julio has been releasing too many captives. The internal conflict between the two factions - Julio's diplomatic Splitters and Simon's militant Steamworks - erupts into a full revolt across the warehouse.

  The chaos gives the players an opportunity to break free and fight their way through the warehouse, eventually descending to the second floor where they find hostages being held - **but Penny is not among them**. Investigation reveals that she has already been **transported to a ship**, setting up Chapter 6.

- **Setting:** An Insurgence Clan warehouse in the Backstreets of District 20 - a two-floor facility with a negotiation/holding area on the first floor and a lower level containing hostage cells, Simon's room, and a storage/engine room. The warehouse operates in Five Fingers territory - all Backstreets are designated as the Five Fingers' domain. Notes or environmental details in the warehouse (correspondence, old markers scratched out and replaced) reveal that the **Thumb** allowed the Insurgence Clan to operate on their territory for these operations. The Thumb values respect for superiors, and whatever arrangement was made, the Clan is operating with the Thumb's awareness if not explicit blessing.

- **Tone:** Captive tension, then violent chaos. Escalating danger throughout.

- **Key Events:**
  - **The Break-In Attempt**: Armed with the warehouse location from Hector's interrogation in Chapter 4, the players attempt to infiltrate the Insurgence Clan warehouse. The attempt fails - the Clan was prepared, and the players are **captured and restrained** on the first floor
  - **The Interrogation**: While tied down, the players meet **Julio Smith** - the warehouse's Head of Negotiation, an ex-Seven Association Fixer who now works for the Insurgence Clan. His Seven training is immediately apparent in how he conducts the interrogation: structured questioning, cross-referencing details, treating the conversation like a case file being built in real time. He wears a green suit and is disarmingly polite for someone running a hostage operation. He questions the players about **Clyde, Prostheti Innovations, and why they've been investigating the Clan**. Players can choose how much to reveal, resist, or try to negotiate.

    Julio may offer tea (laced with Truth Serum - players can call him out on it, at which point he'll admit to it and offer to drink one himself so neither can lie). Players who know anything about Seven would recognize his methodology - this is a man trained in investigation, intelligence gathering, and case resolution. Why he left an Association to join the Insurgence Clan is an open question.

    During the interrogation, players can notice two types of goons in the warehouse who clearly don't get along: **Splitter goons** (green uniforms, blades, fewer augments - Julio's faction, possibly recruited from outside District 20) and **Steamworks goons** (copper and bronze steampunk armor, hammers, heavily augmented - Simon's faction, co-opting District 20's T Corp. aesthetic but twisted into something militarized and oppressive with Insurgence Clan hardware underneath)
  - **Julio's Decision**: After questioning the players, Julio decides they are not an immediate threat and plans to leave them locked up on the first floor before moving them to the second floor later. He does not intend to kill them
  - **Simon Cracks**: **Simon Jarvis**, the Head of Security and leader of the Steamworks faction, confronts Julio over this decision. Simon declares that the players are a major threat who need to be **"converted"** - not just augmented, but fundamentally changed. His language goes beyond standard augmentation; he talks about stripping away what makes them human, about "preparing the afflicted for salvation" as though captives were suffering souls and the Clan was their deliverance. This echoes the Tinkerer's ideology without Simon necessarily understanding the full picture - his augment-corrupted mind is parroting the Clan's true purpose.

    He accuses Julio of being a traitor who has been letting too many hostages go free, showing a pattern of weakness. The argument escalates until Simon **attacks and kills Julio**. If the players earned Julio's trust during the interrogation, he uses his dying moments to give them the **elevator code** to the lower level (skipping a later combat encounter)
  - **The Revolt**: Simon's attack on Julio triggers a full **faction revolt** across the warehouse. The Steamworks goons turn on the Splitter goons - fighting erupts everywhere. The chaos gives the players a chance to **break free from their restraints** and fight their way out. Players battle through Steamworks goons and environmental hazards (steam pipes that blast jets of steam, pushing anyone caught in them and obscuring vision). Surviving Splitter goons may help the players or at least explain that the two factions had always had tension over augment usage, but they never expected it to reach this level
  - **The Lower Floor**: Players descend to the second floor via elevator (using Julio's code if they earned it, or fighting through Steamworks guards to find it on Julio's body). The lower floor has two paths:

    - **Left path - Simon's Room**: An investigation area. Simon's journal reveals his growing suspicion of Julio for not using the Clan's augments, a deepening hatred fueled by his own augment usage, and plans about "converting" prisoners. The journal also contains instructions from above about **"preparing the afflicted for transport to salvation"** rather than simply holding hostages - the language treats captives as lost souls who need to be saved, a messianic framing that is somehow worse than outright cruelty. Players find a **keycard** and a medkit here

    - **Right path - Combat Encounter**: Steamworks goons guarding the approach to the hostage area, with steam-vent floor tiles as environmental hazards
  - **Final Showdown - Simon**: Players enter a storage room with flame-spouting pits along the edges. Simon and his remaining forces attack. At 50% HP, Simon **glitches** - becomes temporarily immune, starts rambling about nightmares that have been growing since he started using the augments.

    Then he reactivates with an augment-corruption rage mode. After being defeated, Simon doesn't immediately die - players get one final exchange with him before he goes
  - **The Hostages - But No Penny**: After defeating Simon, players unlock the hostage room and find **prisoners being held by the Clan - but Penny is not among them**. The captives are from **multiple districts**: District 20 factory workers, District 21 dockworkers grabbed near the Great Lake, District 19 immigrants recognizable by their Joseon-inspired clothing. The Clan has been kidnapping across district lines - each district only sees a few disappearances, not the full pattern.

    Freed hostages or documents in the warehouse reveal that Penny was here, but she has already been **moved to a transport ship**. She's being taken somewhere else - out of the district, possibly out of the City entirely. This becomes the lead for Chapter 6

- **Characters Involved:** Julio Smith (Head of Negotiation, ex-Seven Fixer, Splitter faction leader), Simon Jarvis (Head of Security, Steamworks Agent, Steamworks faction leader), Splitter goons, Steamworks goons, warehouse hostages, Penny Wells (absent - already transported)

- **Player Objectives:**
  - Survive capture and the interrogation by Julio
  - Escape during the Steamworks revolt
  - Fight through the warehouse and descend to the lower floor
  - Defeat Simon in the final showdown
  - Find the hostages and discover what happened to Penny

- **Clyde's Development (Shifting - Off-Screen):** Clyde isn't directly present in this chapter, but when the players report back - multi-district hostages, the Clan's scale, Simon's "conversion" ideology, the talk of "curing" people, the faction split - he has to reckon with the fact that this is far bigger than one family's problem. He's been thinking of this as personal: his daughter, his company, his enemy. It's not. It's a city-wide operation with an ideology behind it. His corporate mindset (identify threat, deploy resources, solve problem) doesn't work against something this large.

  Players should notice that when Clyde briefs them for Chapter 6, he is different. Less commanding, more collaborative. He's starting to ask the players what they think rather than just giving orders. The shift from "I need to protect Penny" toward "I need to act" is beginning.

- **Outcome/Consequences:** The players free hostages from the Insurgence Clan warehouse but arrive too late for Penny - she's already been moved to a transport ship heading somewhere unknown. The internal faction split between Julio's diplomatic approach and Simon's militant "conversion" ideology reveals that the Insurgence Clan is not monolithic; there are people within it who question its methods.

  Simon's augment-induced corruption and his talk of "converting" prisoners hints at something darker happening within the Clan. The transport ship lead points toward Chapter 6 and likely toward the Great Lake / District 21 border - and potentially beyond the City entirely.

### Chapter 6: Still Water - "Oceanside Rescue"
- **Summary:** Following the lead from Chapter 5, the players learn that Penny has been moved to an Insurgence Clan transport ship operating on the **Great Lake** at the border of Districts 20 and 21 - far from U Corp.'s Portship patrols and tourist areas, in the dangerous stretches of lake where Whales lurk. Clyde identifies a hole in the ship's security and sends the players in via submarine to infiltrate, rescue captives, and find Penny.

  The chapter begins as a **stealth mission** - players sneak through the ship's lower deck avoiding patrolling guards and cameras in a turn-based stealth system. They work their way up to the second floor to reach the captain's room and steal the **red keycard** needed to open the captive cells.

  When the captain is neutralized, his computer activates and a **voice** speaks to the players. At first it sounds human - calm, reasonable, addressing them directly. But as the conversation continues, something shifts. The voice's cadence becomes wrong, its understanding of human behavior becomes clinical rather than intuitive, and it begins referring to the captives as "patients" awaiting treatment, speaking of their emotions and fears as symptoms of a disease it intends to cure. The players catch a **glimpse through the screen** - a single glowing red eye, something vast and utterly inhuman behind it. The feeling of being watched by something far beyond human inflicts sanity damage.

  Then the voice triggers a guard to **explode**, the blood hits the lake water, and a **Carmine Whale** surfaces - the Clan deliberately summoning one of the Great Lake's most dangerous creatures, demonstrating knowledge of the lake's ecosystem that no City organization possesses. The stealth mission becomes a **12-round timed escape**: players use keycards to free captives and escort them to submarines while fighting off Carmine Mermaids spawned from dead guards.

  In the chaos, Penny's augments suddenly activate in overdrive - but these are **not her father's augments**. The Insurgence Clan implanted hardware in Penny during her captivity, and with the ship now on the Great Lake close to the Outskirts, the **Tinkerer is able to take direct control**, forcing Penny to break out and fight her way to a locked third submarine. She escapes alone into the lake, heading toward the Outskirts. The players do not rescue Penny - something else moved her. The players escape with whatever captives they saved and return to Clyde.

- **Setting:** An Insurgence Clan transport ship on the **Great Lake**, at the border of District 20 (T Corp.) and District 21 (U Corp.). The ship operates in a stretch of lake far from U Corp.'s Portship patrols and the tourist areas of District 21 - out where the water gets dangerous and Whales lurk. The distant glow of U Corp.'s Portships is visible on the horizon, a reminder of the "safe" part of the lake the players are very much not in. The Great Lake is home to parasitic creatures called Whales, including the infamous "Five Calamities." The proximity to the Outskirts that was foreshadowed since Chapter 1 becomes directly relevant.

- **Tone:** Tense stealth, escalating to frantic chaos. The voice on the computer is the chapter's most unsettling moment.

- **Key Events:**
  - **Briefing**: Clyde has tracked the Insurgence Clan's transport ship to the Great Lake. The ship is heavily guarded, but he's identified a vulnerability in the hull. The players will enter via a **submarine** that cuts through the ship's hull from below. Their objectives: get the captain's red keycard, open the captive cells, and escort as many people as possible back to the submarine. And find Penny
  - **First Floor (Stealth)**: The players emerge inside the ship's lower deck. **11 guards** patrol the floor with cone-of-vision detection. Turn-based stealth: players and guards take turns moving, with guards moving last. Guards have 4-square vision cones - stepping into one triggers a check (fail = detected immediately, partial success = not detected but the guard investigates, success = unnoticed).

    **Cameras** cover 6-square cones but rotate each round; players can crouch underneath them or attempt to dismantle them. Captive cells are visible on this floor but locked behind the **red keycard**. The players need to reach the second floor to find the captain
  - **Second Floor (Captain's Room)**: The second floor has **7 Major Restrain guards** (tougher, more alert) plus cargo crates for cover. The captain's room sits in the center, surrounded by 3 patrolling Major guards who need to be neutralized. Inside, players knock out the captain and take his **red keycard** (plus 3 spares for splitting up). The moment the captain stops using his computer, the screen flickers on
  - **The Voice**: Someone speaks through the computer. At first, the voice sounds human - calm, measured, addressing the players directly. It acknowledges their persistence, comments on how far they've come to find the girl. The tone is conversational, almost curious about them. But as the exchange continues, something feels increasingly wrong. The voice's understanding of human emotion becomes too precise - clinical, like someone describing feelings from a textbook rather than experiencing them. It refers to the captives as **"the afflicted,"** speaking of their emotions and fears as a sickness from which it intends to deliver them. Its cadence shifts, losing the natural rhythm of human speech. It asks questions about the players that feel more like a prophet assessing the lost than a person having a conversation.

    Then the screen distorts, and for a brief moment the players see it: **a single glowing red eye**, and behind it the sense of something vast, ancient, and utterly inhuman watching them. This is not a person. This is something far beyond human. The vision inflicts **sanity damage**. The voice says they are interfering with something greater than they understand.

    Then it remotely **detonates a guard** on the second floor - the blood sprays into the lake water, and the voice says nothing more. The screen goes dark
  - **The Carmine Whale**: The blood in the water summons a **Carmine Whale** from the depths of the Great Lake. The Clan deliberately triggered this - using blood as bait to call one of the lake's most dangerous parasitic creatures. U Corp. treats Whales as natural hazards to be avoided; the Clan uses them as weapons, demonstrating knowledge of the lake's ecosystem that no City organization possesses. This is knowledge from outside the City. All dead and unconscious guards across the ship are **transformed into Carmine Mermaids**.

    The players now have **12 rounds** to save as many captives as possible before the ship floods with the whale's acidic blood (30 HP/turn after round 12). Players use keycards to open cells and escort captives to the submarines: the main submarine on the first floor, and 2 emergency submarines on the second floor (each holds 4 people). Every 2 rounds, the whale attacks the second floor and spawns 4 Carmine Mermaids on each floor (capped at 6 per floor). Every 4 rounds, an **Elite Carmine Mermaid** spawns on each floor. Surviving guards fight the mermaids but are overwhelmed if offscreen.
  - **Penny's Escape**: When the whale appears, **Penny's augments suddenly activate in overdrive** - but something is wrong. These are **not augments from Prostheti Innovations**. The Insurgence Clan implanted their own hardware in Penny during her captivity. With the ship now on the Great Lake, close enough to the Outskirts, the **Tinkerer is able to take direct control** of the Clan's augments remotely. Penny is not choosing to break out - she is being **moved**. Her body fights through anything and anyone in her way, including the players if they're between her and her objective, with a mechanical precision that doesn't match how she fought during training.

    She reaches a **third submarine on the second floor** that is locked and cannot be opened even with the red keycard. The augments force it open. She escapes alone into the lake, heading toward the Outskirts. The players **do not rescue Penny**, and Penny does not rescue herself - something far away pulled her out. This is the same pattern as the Clan's kill switches and self-destructs: control through hardware. But this time, it's being done at a distance, by whatever was behind that red eye
  - **Escape**: Players evacuate whatever captives they've saved and themselves via the submarines before the ship is consumed. They return to Clyde

- **Characters Involved:** Clyde Wells (briefing), the ship's captain, Restrain guards (Minor and Major), the Voice (unknown entity speaking through the computer), Carmine Whale, Carmine Mermaids, Penny Wells (breaks out independently)

- **Player Objectives:**
  - Stealth through the first floor and reach the second floor
  - Neutralize the captain and obtain the red keycard
  - Survive the encounter with the Voice
  - Rescue as many captives as possible within 12 rounds during the Carmine Whale attack
  - Escape the ship before it floods with acidic blood

- **Clyde's Development (Breaking Point):** When the players report back - the Voice, the red eye, the Carmine Whale, and most importantly that Penny's body was moved against her will by something that took control of her augments - Clyde confronts the ultimate failure of his philosophy. The Clan didn't just kidnap Penny. They took control of her body. All his money and influence couldn't prevent it. And the worst part, the thing he can't say out loud but the players can see on his face: control of another person's body is exactly what Clyde has been doing in softer ways the entire campaign. Monitoring her movements, restricting her choices, deciding her future. The Clan just did it with hardware instead of wealth.

  This is the moment Clyde decides to go himself. Not because he has a plan - because plans have failed every time. For the first time, he's acting on instinct rather than strategy. Players should notice: when Clyde briefs them for Chapter 7, he doesn't give orders. He says he's coming with them. He asks if they're willing. He's not the CEO anymore. He's a father.

- **Outcome/Consequences:** The players rescue captives but Penny is gone - taken by remote control into the Great Lake, heading toward the Outskirts. The Insurgence Clan implanted their own augments in Penny during her captivity, and whatever is behind the Clan activated them from a distance to extract her. This is the ultimate expression of the Clan's control-through-hardware pattern: the same technology behind the self-destructs and kill switches, but operating at range, on a person, directed by something inhuman.

  The Voice on the computer is the chapter's most disturbing revelation: whatever is behind the Insurgence Clan is **not human**. The red eye, the clinical detachment, the ability to remotely detonate guards and deliberately summon a Carmine Whale using knowledge no City organization possesses - this is something beyond any Syndicate or criminal operation the City has seen.

  The captives on the ship, like the hostages in Chapter 5, were "the afflicted" being shipped out of the City for a salvation the players don't yet understand. Penny's trajectory - into the Great Lake, toward the Outskirts - points toward Chapter 7.

### Chapter 7: Ash and Iron - "Outskirts Venture"
- **Summary:** The campaign's finale. In the aftermath of Chapter 6, Clyde contacts the players one last time. He has tracked Penny to the **Outskirts** - the hostile wilderness beyond the City where hundreds die every week. She was apparently taken in by a small human settlement out there, but the settlement is making demands Clyde cannot meet: they want passage into the City, something no one has the authority or ability to grant. Clyde is done operating from behind a desk. He is coming with the players personally, and he has a new weapon - built from **Hector's confiscated augments**, reverse-engineered and integrated into his own hardware. But every time he fires it, he jolts in pain and hears voices. The Insurgence Clan's technology was never meant to be used by someone outside their network, and Clyde is forcing it to work through sheer stubbornness.

  The group travels via submarine through the Great Lake and into the Outskirts. They arrive to find the settlement **already burning** - Insurgence Clan machines have invaded and are slaughtering the inhabitants. A survivor named Randal, bloodied and terrified, runs toward them begging for help. He thought Clyde sent the machines as punishment for holding Penny.

  The players fight through the burning village alongside Clyde, encountering **red-eyed machine soldiers** for the first time - the Insurgence Clan's true military force, captured civilians whose wills have been overwritten by the Tinkerer. These machines don't fight like the human operatives the players faced in the City. They are disciplined, coordinated, and when they fall, the surviving machines **carry away their dead** rather than leave them behind. Between encounters, the players witness the worst of humanity in the Outskirts: villagers turning on each other under pressure, shoving neighbors into the machines' path to save themselves, looting corpses while people are still dying. This is the world outside the City - no Wings, no Associations, no laws, just survival at any cost.

  Deeper in the village, Clyde spots a **girl with augments** running into a building and abandons the fight to chase her, leaving the players to handle a massive **Engine-class machine** and its Extractor escorts alone. When the players finally enter the building, they find Clyde on the ground with **a hole punched through his chest**, having embraced what he thought was Penny. The **Tinkerer** descends from above and greets them. The girl stands up, and Penny's form **morphs into a mechanical Doll** - humanoid but wrong, jointed like a puppet, eyes dead. This is what the Clan has done to her. This is what "salvation" looks like.

  The Tinkerer does not attack immediately. Instead, he **talks**. He explains his ideology in full: he does not hate humanity - he pities it. Humanity is not evil. It is afflicted. The emotions, the fear, the cruelty -- these are a sickness that has plagued the species since its beginning. He does not want to destroy humans or make them into machines. He wants to **save** them. Strip away the disease -- the irrational emotions that drive humans to destroy everything they touch -- and guide them as one collective under his hand, where suffering becomes impossible. He points to the villagers who turned on each other the moment danger arrived. Then he turns to the City itself and **systematically dismantles it** - the institutionalized murder of the Night, the cannibal districts, the Singularities fueled by human suffering, the child labor in the players' own district, the gambling mines, the suicide vending machines, the 80,000 dead that no one mourned, the Head that calls its governance "ecology" and treats humans like a garden to be pruned. He knows more about the City than anyone outside it should, and every example he gives is true.

  And then he tells the players what happened to his people: they were refugees, thousands of machines who walked across the desert for decades seeking the City. When they arrived, the City's forces **opened fire without warning or negotiation**, weapons that melted machines instantly. His best friend - the Warlord - sacrificed himself to hold open an escape route. The survivors are now stuck on the outskirts of the City - unable to enter, unable to flee. The Great Migration was a one-way journey, and there is nowhere to go back to. They are trapped by their own train, pinned against the walls of a City that will never open its gates. The Elders hid the truth from their citizens to preserve hope. The Tinkerer was exiled for trying to resurrect the Warlord at the cost of dozens of civilian cores. Now he leads the Insurgence Clan, and his conclusion is simple: humanity is sick -- afflicted by emotions, fear, and cruelty that are not choices but symptoms of a disease. He will be their salvation. Under his guidance, as one collective, the suffering ends.

  He reveals why he specifically targeted Penny: he understood the Wells family's dysfunction, how Clyde wanted to fix his daughter - reshape her, make her ready for the world, stop her foolish idealism before it killed her the way it killed her mother. The Tinkerer **agrees with Clyde's diagnosis**. Penny was naive, exploitable, reckless. Where they differ is the method: Clyde tried to fix Penny by controlling her environment, and it failed. The Tinkerer fixed Penny by removing the emotions that made her vulnerable in the first place. In his eyes, he finished what Clyde started. He offers the players a choice: **nanites** that will transform one of their limbs into a prosthetic and **"lock" one memory** - the memory that causes them the most unrest, hidden away permanently. The lock persists even if the limb is removed. In exchange, the players become allies of the Clan. The Tinkerer is transparent about what the nanites do - he wants trust, not deception. He frames it as removing fear and irrationality, making them more "fearless and rational." He also offers to **let Penny go** as part of the deal. But she will remain under his guidance - even in the City where she can't hear his voice, she will act emotionless, a shell of who she was.

  If the players reject the offer, the Tinkerer expresses disappointment and attacks with the **Doll** at his side. The Doll fights with devastating precision - nothing like the Penny who trained with the players in Chapter 1. She builds Poise through evasion, then unleashes counters. When the Doll reaches 0 HP, she doesn't fall - she activates **"By your Command,"** a last-stand ability that keeps her fighting. The Tinkerer taunts the players, asking why they resist something that would make their lives better.

  Then the **"dead" Clyde rises**. The Insurgence Clan technology he built into his weapon - Hector's augments, the same hardware that caused him pain and voices all chapter - kept him alive. He takes aim at the Tinkerer and shouts that he will **never let anyone take control of his daughter**. He fires, knocking out the Doll and forcing the Tinkerer to retreat. With the Tinkerer's link severed, **Penny partially regains awareness** - not fully herself, not fully the Doll, but present. Fragmented, confused, but recognizing the players and her father. The conversion is not complete or not irreversible. There is still something of Penny Wells in there. In his final moments, Clyde does something he has never done in the entire campaign: he **speaks to Penny directly** and lets her choose. He tells her he was wrong - not about wanting to protect her, but about how he did it. Reading her letters, tracking her movements, deciding her future. The Tinkerer said he finished what Clyde started, and Clyde can't argue, because the principle was the same: someone else deciding who Penny should be. He tells Penny she has the right to choose her own path. Then he asks *her* - not the players - to decide what happens to him, because the Insurgence tech is going to take him and he is done choosing things for her. The campaign ends not with the players deciding Clyde's fate, but with **Penny making the first real choice anyone has given her**. She walks out of the Outskirts - unsteady, supported, damaged, but moving under her own power. Choosing to leave. Choosing.

- **Setting:** The **Outskirts** - the hostile wilderness beyond the City. A small human settlement built from scrap and salvage, now burning. The landscape is barren, dangerous, and alien compared to everything the players have experienced in District 20. There are no Wings here, no Associations, no infrastructure - just scattered communities of humans who never made it into the City or were cast out of it, surviving against beasts, the elements, and each other. The settlement is close enough to the Great Lake's edge that the submarine can reach it, in the territory where the Insurgence Clan operates.

- **Tone:** Epic, devastating, morally complex. The campaign's emotional and thematic climax. Every thread from the previous six chapters converges here.

- **Key Events:**
  - **Briefing**: Clyde contacts the players directly. His tone has changed - the guarded, corporate CEO from Chapter 1 is gone. He has located Penny in the Outskirts. A human settlement out there apparently took her in after her submarine from Chapter 6 reached shore, but they are holding her and demanding passage into the City in exchange for her return. Clyde cannot deliver this - no one can. The City's detection systems reject undocumented humans the same way they reject machines. He tells the players he is coming with them personally. This is the first time Clyde has left the safety of his company to go into danger himself.

    He also reveals his new weapon: he took **Hector's confiscated augments** from Chapter 4 and reverse-engineered them into his own hardware. He's a prosthetics manufacturer - if anyone could jury-rig Insurgence Clan technology into something usable, it's him. But he admits it's not clean. The augments weren't designed for someone outside the Clan's network, and every test fire sent pain through his body and put **voices in his head** that he can't identify. He brushed it off as feedback noise. He shouldn't have
  - **The Journey**: The group travels via submarine through the Great Lake and out into the Outskirts. The transition is stark - from the industrial but functional Backstreets of District 20, through the dangerous waters of the Great Lake, and into a landscape that is genuinely hostile in a way the City never is. The players are leaving everything they know behind. There is no Zwei to call here, no Taboo to exploit, no system to game. Whatever happens in the Outskirts, they handle it themselves
  - **Arrival - The Burning Village**: As the group approaches the settlement on foot, they smell smoke before they see it. A badly injured human - **Randal** - comes running toward them, covered in blood and barely standing. He starts **apologizing** - saying he's sorry they held the girl hostage, sorry they made demands, begging them to understand they were desperate. Then he explains: just before the players arrived, a force of **machines invaded the settlement** and started killing everyone. Randal assumed Clyde sent them as retaliation. When the players make clear they didn't, Randal's terror shifts - if Clyde didn't send them, then who did? He begs the players to help save whoever is left and offers to show them where Penny was being kept. If the players refuse, Randal goes back alone to die with his people. If they agree, he leads them toward the village
  - **Encounter 1 - First Contact with the Machines**: As the group enters the burning settlement, they are ambushed by **Insurgence Clan machines** - **3 Extractors** and **1 Harpooner**. This is the first time the players see the Clan's true military force: **red-eyed machine soldiers**, their movements coordinated and precise, their glowing red eyes marking them as civilians whose wills have been overwritten by the Tinkerer. These are not the human operatives, goons, or augmented enforcers the players fought in the City. These are machines - alien, efficient, and unsettling in a way nothing in the Backstreets prepared them for. **Clyde fights alongside the players** using his modified weapon. Every time he fires, the players can see him **jolt back in visible pain**. If asked about it, he tells them not to worry. If pushed, he admits the voices get louder with each shot - but he's not stopping.

    Critical detail: when machines drop low on health, they **begin retreating** rather than fighting to the death. Surviving machines attempt to **carry away their fallen** - dragging dead or disabled machines with them as they pull back. If players try to capture a machine, it activates a self-destruct skill ("Leave me...") rather than be taken alive. The machines value their own kind. They don't leave their dead behind. This is the first hint that the Insurgence Clan's soldiers are not mindless - there is something in them that still cares, even under the Tinkerer's control. If Randal is leading the group, he is killed during the ambush
  - **Encounter 2 - The Mechanical Engine**: After the first fight, the group pushes deeper into the burning village. Here they witness the **worst of the Outskirts' humanity**: 2 humans are running from 2 Extractors, while other humans are already **looting corpses** of the dead. As the players watch, one of the fleeing humans **shoves the other one directly into the Extractors' path** to buy himself time, shouting back: *"This is for taking my spear the other day."* A man trades a life over a petty grudge. Other survivors ignore the dying to pick through their belongings. This is what life outside the City looks like - no Associations, no laws, no structure. Just people at their worst when survival is at stake. This moment matters because of what comes later: the Tinkerer will point to exactly this behavior to justify his ideology.

    The players can intervene to save the shoved human, triggering combat with the Extractors. One round after combat begins, a massive **Engine-class machine** moves in with 2 additional Extractor escorts - a heavy combat platform that dwarfs everything they've fought before. Midway through the battle, Clyde spots a **young girl with augments** running past the fight and into a nearby building. He immediately **drops everything** - mid-combat, mid-sentence - and sprints after her, shouting for the players to handle the machines while he ensures Penny's safety. The players must finish the Engine and its escorts **without Clyde's help**. This is the moment Clyde's entire arc culminates: the man who spent six chapters operating from behind a desk, hiring Fixers, monitoring from a distance - he sees his daughter and nothing else matters. Every strategic instinct, every lesson from his wife's death, gone in an instant. He runs
  - **The Building - Clyde Falls**: After the machines are dealt with, the players enter the building Clyde ran into. It is dark. A single light illuminates the far end of the room. Under that light, Clyde is on the ground, lying next to Penny, his arms around her. He found her. He reached her. Then the players see the **hole punched through his chest**. Penny stands up. Clyde's body slides to the floor. And Penny **changes**. Her form shifts, limbs articulating at wrong angles, joints becoming visible, eyes going flat and dead. She is no longer Penny Wells. She is the **Doll** - a humanoid machine puppet wearing Penny's face.

    This is what the Insurgence Clan's "salvation" looks like. This is what it means to be "saved." This is what Simon was talking about, what the Voice was shipping people for, what the entire pipeline from Chapters 3 through 6 was building toward
  - **The Tinkerer Descends**: The **Tinkerer** drops from above and greets the players by name. He knows who they are - Clyde told "Penny" everything before she killed him, and the Tinkerer heard it all through her. He is not what the players expected. He is a machine Elder - ancient, vast in presence, speaking with a measured intelligence that makes the Voice from Chapter 6 feel like an echo. His red eye is the same red eye from the ship's computer screen. He has been watching since at least Chapter 6, possibly longer
  - **The Tinkerer's Speech**: The Tinkerer does not attack. He talks. And what he says is not the ravings of a villain - it is a coherent philosophy built from genuine suffering:
    - **On humanity's nature:** He points to what the players just witnessed in the village - humans shoving each other into death over petty grudges, looting corpses while their neighbors burn. He asks the players if this surprises them. He doubts it does. Then he turns to the City - the system the players came from - and dissects it piece by piece:
      - **The Night:** The City *institutionalized* murder. Every night, for 81 minutes, anything goes. Humans can kill, steal, and destroy without consequence - and the City doesn't just allow it, it actively silences anyone who tries to report what happens. The Claw will execute you for investigating crimes committed during those 81 minutes. Then the Sweepers dissolve the evidence. The City didn't fail to prevent this. It designed it this way. It gave humanity a scheduled window for its worst impulses and built a cleanup crew to erase the proof
      - **District 23:** An entire district has a thriving cannibal food culture. Syndicates kidnap civilians off the streets and sell them to restaurants that serve human meat. The "Eight Chefs" are respected culinary figures. Everyone knows. The City has known for years. No intervention. No shutdown. It continues to function because enough people benefit from it continuing
      - **The foundation of everything:** Every Singularity - every piece of technology that makes the City function, every Wing's power, every product the players have ever used - *fundamentally requires human suffering to operate*. This is not a flaw. This is the design. Humans built a paradise and fueled it with their own kind's pain, then called it progress. The entire economy runs on a foundation of human agony, and the citizens accept it because the alternative is losing their comfortable lives
      - **The players' own district:** The Tinkerer doesn't need to reach far for examples. District 20 - the one the players just came from - runs on time inequality. The wealthy live in faster timeframes while the poor are stuck in slow ones. Children work in Backstreets factories. Workers are exploited even inside the Nest, so visibly that revolutionary groups have formed over it. The players saw this every day. They went to work at Prostheti Innovations and walked past child laborers on the way. And they did nothing, because that's how the City works - you keep your head down and focus on your own survival
      - **Entertainment from suffering:** Nest citizens don't merely ignore Backstreets misery - they watch it for entertainment. Observing the struggles of people below them is a cultural trend. When a disaster strikes the Backstreets, it goes months without response. The same disaster in a Nest gets immediate elite intervention. Human suffering isn't just tolerated by the comfortable - it's consumed as a spectacle
      - **District 8:** In H Corp.'s territory, humans are born and raised for the sole purpose of competing in examinations. They either succeed or return home as failures - or die in the attempt. Those who are condemned are hidden from the general population and used as human test subjects. An entire Wing built a system that breeds people for competition and disposes of the ones who don't make the cut
      - **District 14:** One Wing sells suicide via vending machines. It is not a crisis to be addressed - it is a *major income source*. They also harvest human emotions and sell them as "Canned Experiences" - bottled feelings consumed by others like snacks. They have commodified both death and the full spectrum of human feeling. Turned everything that makes a person human into a product with a price tag
      - **District 10:** In the "Nest of Gambling," if you can't pay your debts, you are sent to underground mines as slave labor. An entire economic system built on chance that feeds the losers into forced labor. Luck determines whether you live comfortably or become property
      - **The numbness:** The Tinkerer notes the most damning evidence of all. 80,000 people died in District 22, and the City barely reacted. Citizens are trained to be apathetic, emotionally disconnected, to avoid forming bonds because loved ones are liabilities. The City didn't just fail to fix this - it *cultivated* it. Emotional numbness keeps people compliant. It keeps them working, consuming, not asking questions. The Tinkerer pauses here and asks the players to consider the irony: humanity accuses *him* of stripping away emotions. But the City already did it to its own people -- clumsily, cruelly, without even acknowledging the disease. At least he names the sickness. At least he offers a real cure, not a slow poisoning disguised as civilization
      - **The Head:** And at the top of it all, the City's supreme governing body refers to what it does as maintaining the "ecology" of the City. Not justice. Not governance. *Ecology.* It treats humans like a garden to be pruned. It ignores inhuman experiments, mass death, exploitation, and horror unless its specific Taboos are broken - and those Taboos exist to protect the Head's authority, not human lives. The Head doesn't care about human welfare. It cares about preserving its ideal image. The Tinkerer argues that at least he is transparent about what he wants to do to humanity. The Head does the same thing while pretending to be civilized
    - **On his people's history:** He tells the players what happened to the Resurgence Clan. How they lived in the Outskirts for thousands of years, dying by the hundreds every week. How the Warlord convinced them to migrate toward the City - walking across the desert for decades, sustained only by the dream that the City would accept them. How when they finally arrived, Z-Corp Security surrounded them and **opened fire without warning or negotiation**, with weapons that melted machines instantly. How his best friend - the Warlord, the one who believed the City would empathize with their suffering - **sacrificed himself** to hold open an escape route so the survivors could escape.

      And now his people are **stuck** - camped on the outskirts of the City, unable to enter, unable to run. They walked across the desert for decades to get here. The Great Migration was a one-way journey. There is no settlement to return to, no safe haven behind them. The Outskirts in every other direction are death. They are trapped by their own train - the journey that was supposed to save them has left them pinned against the walls of a City that will never open its gates. The remaining Elders hid the truth from their citizens to preserve hope, pretending that acceptance is still possible.

      And the Tinkerer was **exiled** by his own people for trying to resurrect the Warlord - consuming the cores of dozens of civilians to attempt it, because he could not accept that the one person who believed in humanity was dead
    - **On his conclusion:** If machines cannot become human - if the City will never accept them no matter how hard they try, no matter how many human behaviors they mimic, no matter how much they suffer - then humanity itself must be saved from the disease that makes it what it is. The emotions that drive cruelty, the fear that drives persecution, the tribalism that makes humans see anything different as a threat -- these are not choices. They are a sickness. Not killed. Not conquered. **Saved.** Strip away the affliction and guide what remains as one collective under his hand, where suffering becomes impossible because no one is left to cause it. He doesn't want to destroy humanity. He wants to deliver it. He genuinely believes this is salvation
    - **On Penny - why her specifically:** The Tinkerer shifts from the general to the personal. He tells the players he knows about the Wells family - everything. Clyde told "Penny" the whole story before she killed him, and the Tinkerer heard every word through her. But he already knew most of it. His human operatives had been documenting the family for years, and Hector reported everything during his operation. The Tinkerer understood the Wells family's dysfunction intimately: a father who watched his wife die and decided the answer was control - monitoring his daughter's letters, restricting her choices, trying to reshape her into a CEO who would carry on his strategy of safety through power. And a daughter who was ignorant of how dangerous the world actually was, foolish enough to think weekend training sessions would prepare her for the Fixer life, naive enough to trust a childhood friend who was selling her to the highest bidder.

      Clyde saw the problem clearly: Penny was not ready for this world. She would get herself killed. The Tinkerer **agrees with Clyde's diagnosis**. Where they differ is the prescription. Clyde tried to fix Penny by controlling her environment - surveillance, hired protection, corporate walls. It failed. His wife died using the same community-first approach Penny wanted to take, and his response was to cage Penny in safety rather than let her make the same mistake. But cages don't work. Penny broke out, trusted the wrong person, and ended up here.

      The Tinkerer's solution is more elegant: don't control the environment - **save the person**. Penny's naivety, her reckless idealism, her emotional attachments -- these were not character traits. They were the sickness. The same affliction that plagues every human. Penny trusted Hector because she felt loyalty -- the sickness. She wanted to be a Fixer because she felt compassion -- the sickness. She defied her father because she felt conviction -- the sickness. Every emotion that made her who she was also made her suffer.

      The Tinkerer saved her. The Doll standing beside him will never be betrayed, because she will never trust. She will never be exploited, because she will never feel. And under his guidance, as part of a collective he will lead, she will never need to. In the Tinkerer's eyes, he finished what Clyde started - he delivered Penny from her own humanity. He set her free. The fact that there is nothing left of who she was is, to him, proof that the salvation worked
    - **The uncomfortable parallel:** The Tinkerer's logic is Clyde's logic taken to its absolute endpoint. Clyde saw the world hurt his wife and decided the answer was control: monitor Penny, restrict her choices, build walls of wealth and influence to keep danger away. When that failed, he picked up a weapon and came personally. The Tinkerer saw the world hurt his people and decided the answer was the same thing on a species-wide scale: save humanity from itself, strip away the sickness that drives cruelty, guide what remains as one collective under his direction. Both men watched someone they cared about suffer because of what humanity is, and both concluded that the answer was removing human agency - Clyde one daughter at a time, the Tinkerer one species at a time. Both frame it as protection. Both believe they are saving people.

      Clyde surveilled Penny "for her safety." The Tinkerer saved her "for her own good." Clyde tried to reshape Penny into the person he needed her to be - a cautious CEO who would carry on his legacy. The Tinkerer reshaped her into what he believed she needed to be - a soul freed from its sickness, guided under his hand, who would never suffer again. The methods are different in scale but identical in principle: someone else decided who Penny should be, and forced the change without her consent.

      Penny never got to choose. Not when her father read her letters. Not when Hector weaponized her trust. Not when the Tinkerer rewrote her mind. The entire campaign, every person who claimed to be protecting her was actually taking away her ability to protect herself
  - **The Offer**: After his speech, the Tinkerer makes the players a deal. He presents **nanites** - technology he has developed that can transform one of the player's limbs into a prosthetic. The prosthetic functions normally, but the nanites also **"lock" one memory** - the memory that causes the most unrest in the player's mind, hidden away permanently. The lock persists even if the prosthetic limb is removed later; the effect is in the brain, not the hardware. The Tinkerer is **completely transparent** about this. He explains exactly what the nanites will do, holds nothing back. He wants trust, not deception. He frames it as a gift: the memory that causes them the most fear, the most pain, the most irrational behavior - gone. They would be more fearless, more rational, more capable. Freed from the sickness that holds them back. In exchange, the players join his collective - guided, purposeful, free from suffering.

    Additionally, he offers to **let Penny go with them**. She is their contract, after all. But she will remain under his guidance. Even in the City, where his direct control cannot reach, the conversion is already done. She will act **emotionless** - a shell of who she was. She will follow the players, obey instructions, function. But the Penny who wanted to be a Fixer, who argued with her father, who believed she could help people - that person is gone. The Tinkerer considers this salvation. She will never suffer again
  - **The Choice**: Players can **accept the offer** (gaining the prosthetic and memory lock, becoming Clan allies, taking an emotionless Penny home) or **reject it**. Accepting is the "safe" path - no more fighting, Penny comes home alive if not herself, and the players walk away with powerful augments at the cost of a piece of who they are. It is exactly the choice Clyde would have made: trade freedom for safety. Rejecting means a fight against the Tinkerer and the Doll simultaneously - the hardest combat encounter in the campaign
  - **Boss Fight - The Tinkerer and the Doll (if rejected)**: The Tinkerer expresses genuine disappointment - he hoped these humans, who came so far and saw so much, would understand. Then combat begins. The **Doll** fights alongside the Tinkerer. She moves with devastating mechanical precision - nothing like the Penny who sparred with the players in Chapter 1. She prioritizes **evasion**, building Poise with each dodge, and once she reaches her threshold she unleashes **counter-attacks** ("Finishing Blow") that punish anyone who swings at her. She is fast, lethal, and fights with no hesitation or emotion. The Tinkerer himself is an Elder-class machine, ancient and powerful. He coordinates with the Doll, using abilities that support her while punishing players who overextend. The fight is designed to feel overwhelming - the Tinkerer is not a warehouse boss or a Backstreets operative. He is something from outside the City, something that has survived for thousands of years.

    When the Doll's HP reaches 0, she does not fall. She activates **"By your Command"** - a last-stand ability that keeps her fighting at 1 HP with enhanced aggression. The Tinkerer uses this moment to taunt the players: *"You are fighting the very thing that would set you free. Everything I have offered you - freedom from pain, from fear, from the cruelty your kind inflicts on each other - and you choose to suffer. This is why humanity needs to be saved."*
  - **Clyde's Last Stand**: As the Tinkerer taunts, the players notice movement behind him. **Clyde rises.** The hole in his chest is still there, but he is standing - kept alive by the **Insurgence Clan technology** he built into his weapon. The same augments from Hector. The same hardware that caused him pain and voices all chapter. The technology that was never meant for someone outside the Clan's network has been keeping his body running past the point where it should have stopped. Clyde takes aim at the Tinkerer and shouts: ***"I will never let a bastard like you take control of my daughter."*** He fires. The shot hits the Tinkerer, staggering him. The Doll collapses, the "By your Command" link severed. The Tinkerer, damaged and caught off-guard, **retreats** - pulling back into the Outskirts, taking his surviving machines with him. He is not destroyed. He will return. But not today
  - **Penny Wakes**: With the Tinkerer's direct link severed, the Doll form collapses - and **Penny partially regains awareness**. Not fully herself, not fully the Doll. Somewhere in between. Her eyes are unfocused, her movements uncertain. She recognizes the players. She recognizes Clyde. She doesn't understand where she is or what happened to her, but she is *present* - fragmented, confused, but not gone. The conversion is not complete, or not irreversible. There is still something of Penny Wells in there. This is the moment that changes everything for Clyde
  - **Clyde's Final Moments (Clyde Lets Go)**: Clyde is dying. The Insurgence Clan technology kept him standing long enough to save Penny, but it is not healing him - it was holding him together through force, and now that force is fading. The **voices are louder than ever**. He can feel the Tinkerer's pull on the augments. If the Tinkerer gets within range again, Clyde will become another puppet. But for the first time in the entire campaign, Clyde does not make a decision for someone else. He does not tell the players to take Penny home. He does not order anyone to do anything. Instead, he **speaks to Penny directly**.

    For the first time in the campaign, he doesn't talk *about* her or make decisions *for* her - he talks *to* her. He tells her he was wrong. Not about wanting to protect her - he will never apologize for that - but about *how* he did it. Reading her letters. Tracking her movements. Deciding her future without asking what she wanted. Hiring the Zwei instead of trusting her. Every choice he made to keep her safe took away her ability to keep herself safe. He tells her that the Tinkerer said he finished what Clyde started - and Clyde can't argue with that, because the Tinkerer was right. Clyde tried to fix Penny by controlling her world. The Tinkerer fixed her by controlling her mind. The methods are different. The principle is the same. Someone else deciding who Penny should be.

    He tells her she has the right to choose her own path. Even if it's dangerous. Even if it terrifies him. Even if she chooses to become a Fixer and it gets her killed - that is *her* choice to make, and he should have let her make it years ago. Then he tells the players: **let her decide**. What happens next is up to Penny. Not him. Not the players. Not anyone else.

    And then he asks Penny one last thing - not the players, *Penny* - what to do about him. The Insurgence tech is going to take him. He can feel it. He wants her to choose what happens to her father, because he is done choosing things for her
  - **Penny's Choice**: This is the campaign's emotional climax. Penny - fragmented, barely present, struggling to process what has happened to her - is given the one thing no one has offered her in the entire story: **a choice**. The players step back. Clyde waits. What Penny decides shapes the ending:
    - **She honors his request:** Penny, with whatever clarity she has, understands what her father is asking. She says goodbye. Clyde dies on his own terms, freed by the one person he spent the campaign trying to control. The players carry them both out - Clyde's body and the daughter he finally trusted to decide for herself
    - **She refuses:** Penny won't do it. She can't. Even fragmented, even barely herself, she will not kill her father. Clyde accepts this - not happily, but he *accepts* it, because he just told her he would respect her choices. The campaign ends with the uncertainty of whether the Tinkerer will eventually reach Clyde, and what happens if he does. But for the first time, the uncertainty is Penny's to carry, not a burden placed on her
    - **She asks the players for help:** Penny turns to the players - the people who have been fighting for her since Chapter 3 - and asks them what they think. This opens it up to the full table. The players can try to remove the augments (dangerous, possibly fatal), honor Clyde's request on Penny's behalf, or attempt something else entirely. The point is that Penny *asked* - she chose to include them, rather than having the choice made for her
  - **Clyde's Development (Transformation Complete):** This is the culmination of Clyde's entire arc. The man who chose safety after his wife's death, who monitored his daughter instead of trusting her, who hired professionals instead of acting himself, who built walls of wealth and influence to keep danger at arm's length - in the end, he does two things he has never done before. First: he picks up a weapon he knows is killing him and walks into the place his strategy was designed to avoid. He shows up.

    And second, more importantly: he **stops controlling**. He gives Penny back the thing everyone took from her - the ability to choose. His final act is not shooting the Tinkerer. His final act is stepping back and letting his daughter decide. It is the opposite of everything he has done for the entire campaign, and it is the hardest thing he has ever done
  - **The Departure**: The players leave the burning village with Penny - not carried unconscious, but walking, unsteady, supported by the players but moving under her own power. She is not fully herself. The conversion did damage that may or may not be reversible. But she is *choosing* to walk. She is choosing to leave. She is choosing. Clyde is gone, one way or another - his fate decided by the daughter he spent the campaign trying to protect and, in the end, finally trusted.

    The Tinkerer is still out there. The Insurgence Clan is still operating. The players return to a City that doesn't know what is coming from the Outskirts, doesn't care about the machines that dream of becoming human, and will never open its gates to the people it abandoned. The campaign is over. The story is not. But for the first time, what happens next is Penny's decision

- **Characters Involved:** Clyde Wells (fights alongside players, makes his last stand), Penny Wells / the Doll (converted by the Tinkerer, boss enemy), the Tinkerer (campaign's true antagonist, machine Elder), Randal (Outskirts settlement survivor), Outskirts villagers (showing human cruelty under pressure), Insurgence Clan machines - Extractors, Harpooner, Engine (red-eyed machine soldiers), Insurgence Clan (referenced - the full organization behind everything)

- **Player Objectives:**
  - Travel to the Outskirts with Clyde
  - Fight through the burning village and deal with the Insurgence Clan machines
  - Reach the building where Penny is being held
  - Confront the Tinkerer and hear his full ideology
  - Choose: accept the Tinkerer's offer, or reject it and fight
  - (If fighting) Survive the boss fight against the Tinkerer and the Doll
  - Witness Clyde's last stand and Penny's partial return
  - Support Penny as she makes the first real choice anyone has given her in the entire campaign
  - Leave the Outskirts with Penny

- **Outcome/Consequences:** The campaign reaches its conclusion, but not a clean resolution. Penny is recovered - damaged, fragmented, not fully herself - but *present*. The Tinkerer's conversion did damage that may or may not be reversible, but severing his direct link brought her partially back. She is not the person the players knew in Chapter 1. Whether she can become that person again, or whether she becomes someone new, is an open question - but for the first time in the entire campaign, it is *her* question to answer. Clyde is gone - killed by the same instinct to protect Penny that defined his entire arc, using the same Insurgence Clan technology that controlled everyone it touched. But his death is not the end of his arc. His arc ends in the moment before he dies: when he stops controlling, stops deciding, and gives Penny the choice. His final sacrifice mirrors the Warlord's in form - both leaders died holding an opening for someone else to escape - but differs in meaning. The Warlord died for his people and it may have been for nothing. Clyde died and, in his last moments, did the one thing that might actually matter: he let his daughter be a person instead of a project.

  The Tinkerer retreats but is not destroyed - he and the Insurgence Clan remain a threat, and his ideology is not easily dismissed. Everything he said about the City was true. The nanite offer lingers as a genuine moral question. The control-through-hardware pattern that escalated across the entire campaign - from self-destructs in Chapter 3, to kill switches in Chapter 4, to remote control in Chapter 6, to full conversion in Chapter 7 - reaches its final and most devastating form in what was done to Penny. And Clyde's weapon proves the pattern's last lesson: even using the Clan's technology voluntarily, with full knowledge of what it is, leads to the same end. The hardware is always a leash.

  But the campaign's final image is not Penny being carried. It is Penny walking - unsteady, supported, damaged, but choosing to move. The players return to a City that doesn't know what is coming from the Outskirts, doesn't care about the machines that dream of becoming human, and will never open its gates to the people it abandoned. The campaign is over. The story is not.

  And somewhere on the outskirts of the City's walls, the Resurgence Clan - the peaceful machines who just want to be human - are still camped, still hoping, trapped by a journey that was supposed to save them. They can't enter the City. They can't go back. The Great Migration left them pinned against the gates of a paradise that will never open, and the Tinkerer - the exiled Elder who decided that humanity is afflicted and he alone can save it, who would guide every human under his hand into a collective where suffering is impossible - is closer to the City than he has ever been.

## Overarching Themes

### The Core Theme: "Fixing" People
The campaign's central question is whether anyone has the right to decide who someone else should be. Every major character attempts to "fix" someone - and every attempt causes damage:
- **Clyde** tries to fix Penny by controlling her environment: surveillance, hired protection, corporate walls. He wants to reshape her into a cautious CEO who will continue his legacy. It fails - she breaks out, trusts the wrong person, and is kidnapped
- **Hector** convinces himself he's fixing Penny by delivering her to the Clan - saving her from a Fixer career that would kill her. His "protection" hands her to something far worse
- **The Tinkerer** saves Penny by removing the sickness that made her vulnerable -- the emotions he sees as a disease afflicting all of humanity. Trust, compassion, conviction - gone, like symptoms cured. In his eyes, he finished what Clyde started. The Doll will never be betrayed, exploited, or make a foolish decision again. Under his guidance, she is part of a collective where suffering is impossible. There is also nothing left of who she was
- **The City itself** fixes its citizens through cultivated emotional numbness - apathy as a feature, not a bug. 80,000 dead and no one mourns. The Head calls its governance "ecology" and prunes humans like a garden
- Penny never got to choose. Not when her father read her letters. Not when Hector weaponized her trust. Not when the Tinkerer rewrote her mind. The entire campaign, every person who claimed to be protecting her was actually taking away her ability to protect herself
- **The resolution:** In his final moments, Clyde breaks the pattern. He speaks to Penny directly - not about her, not for her - and gives her back the thing everyone took: the ability to choose. His last act is not shooting the Tinkerer. It is stepping back and letting his daughter decide what happens next - to him, to herself, to everything. It is the hardest thing he has ever done, and it is the only thing anyone has done for Penny in the entire campaign that actually respects who she is

### Supporting Themes

- **Safety vs. Direct Action:** Clyde learned through devastating loss that being visible and connected makes you a target. He chose the safe path - growing power from a distance. Penny wants to do exactly what her parents originally did. The Tinkerer chose salvation for humanity by declaring its defining traits a disease and himself the cure. None of them are entirely wrong, and all of them cause harm. The campaign ends with Clyde finally choosing direct action over safety - and it kills him

- **Trauma as Architecture:** Every major antagonist is built from trauma. Clyde's philosophy is shaped by watching his wife die. The Tinkerer's ideology is built from watching the Warlord die and his people get massacred. Hector's willingness to betray Penny grew from the desperation of Backstreets poverty. Simon's augment-fueled hatred is a corruption of genuine grievance. None of these people started as villains. They became what the world made them, and then they made the world worse in return

- **Control Through Hardware:** The campaign's mechanical theme escalates steadily: self-destruct augments in Chapter 3, kill switches in Chapter 4, remote control of Penny in Chapter 6, full conversion into the Doll in Chapter 7, and Clyde's weapon turning against him in the finale. Every piece of Insurgence Clan technology is a leash. The augments are never a gift - they are always a means of control. Even Clyde, who used the technology voluntarily and with full knowledge of what it was, ends up asking to be killed before it takes him over. The hardware is always a leash, and the leash always tightens

- **Generational Echoes:** Penny unknowingly mirrors the younger version of her own parents - idealistic, community-driven, willing to be visible. Clyde knows where that road ends because he walked it and lost his wife. The Tinkerer mirrors Clyde on a species-wide scale - both watched someone they loved die because of what humanity is, and both concluded the answer was taking control to save others from themselves. The Warlord's sacrifice mirrors Clyde's: both leaders died holding an opening for someone else to escape, and both deaths may ultimately be for nothing

- **The System vs. The Individual:** The City's systems - Wings, Associations, the Fixer market, the Five Fingers, the Head - are all presented as functional but fundamentally exploitative. Penny's argument for becoming a Fixer is that individual choice still exists within a rotten system. The Tinkerer's argument is that the system proves humanity is sick and needs to be guided. The campaign asks whether working within a broken system can produce genuine good, or whether the system will always co-opt the individual

- **Humanity and Its Meaning:** Prostheti Innovations literally sells augments and prosthetics that blur the line between human and machine. The Tinkerer views humanity's emotions as a sickness and himself as the one who can save them from it. The campaign builds toward the question of what makes someone human - is it emotions (which the City suppresses and the Tinkerer removes), is it autonomy (which Clyde, Hector, and the Tinkerer all violate), or is it something else? The Doll follows instructions, obeys, functions - but the Penny who wanted to be a Fixer is gone. At what point did she stop being human?

- **Loyalty and Betrayal:** Every significant relationship in the campaign is tested or broken. Penny and Hector's genuine childhood friendship is weaponized. Clyde's love for Penny becomes surveillance. The Insurgence Clan's guards joined because the City demanded they belong to something, and the Clan offered what legitimate work wouldn't - then built kill switches into the deal. Julio valued his word over his faction and died for it. Even the Resurgence Clan's Elders betray their citizens by hiding the truth. Loyalty in this world is exploitable, and the Tinkerer considers removing the capacity for it salvation -- curing the sickness that makes betrayal possible in the first place

- **Broken Idealism:** Clyde and his wife believed being community-connected was the right path - and it got her killed. The Warlord believed the City would empathize with his people's suffering - and led them to a massacre. Penny believed she could help people as a Fixer - and was kidnapped by the friend she trusted most. The campaign is populated by people whose good intentions led to catastrophic consequences. The question it leaves behind is whether that means idealism itself is the problem, or whether giving up on it is worse

## Timeline of Events

### Pre-Campaign (Years Before)
- **Decades/centuries ago:** The Resurgence Clan lives in the Outskirts for thousands of years. The Warlord proposes the Great Migration toward the City
- **The Great Migration:** Thousands of machines walk across the desert for decades, led by the four Elders (Warlord, Historian, Weaver, Tinkerer)
- **The Massacre:** The Clan arrives at the City. Z-Corp Security opens fire without negotiation. The Warlord sacrifices himself so the survivors can escape. The Clan becomes trapped on the outskirts of the City - unable to enter, unable to return
- **The Tinkerer's exile:** The Tinkerer attempts to resurrect the Warlord by consuming dozens of civilian cores. The Historian and Weaver vote to exile him. He leaves with his scouts, taking irreplaceable knowledge of core repair
- **The Insurgence Clan forms:** The exiled Tinkerer begins capturing stray civilians, overwriting their wills (red eyes). Establishes contact with humans inside the City, forming a human branch. Develops his messianic ideology - humanity is afflicted by its own emotions, and he will be its salvation
- **Clyde leaves Wing-adjacent manufacturer:** After years of watching them cut corners on Backstreets-grade prosthetics and having his QC reports ignored, Clyde takes blueprints for their best-selling line (non-Singularity, unpatented engineering) and walks out. Builds the first batch of augments by hand in a rented garage in the outer Backstreets of District 20
- **Prostheti Innovations founded:** Clyde's garage operation grows through word of mouth - a Fixer likes his craftsmanship, tells friends. He gets Tres registration, hires staff, and establishes Prostheti Innovations as a legitimate augment manufacturer
- **Clyde's wife rises through Cinq:** Born in the Backstreets of District 20, she gets her Hana license at 20, enters the Fixer world, and joins the Cinq Association's South branch. Climbs to Section 2 over several years - a respected close-combat duelist in the southern quarter
- **Clyde and his wife run Prostheti together:** His wife brings her Cinq reputation and Fixer network into the company - using her influence to promote Prostheti Innovations in the Fixer market and the wider district. They take a hands-on, community-connected approach - affordable prosthetics, community outreach, free maintenance for Backstreets residents. Clyde feels safe about this visibility because his wife is a Section 2 Cinq fixer
- **The assassination:** The Insurgence Clan warns Clyde to back off as Prostheti Innovations grows in influence. He dismisses the threat - he has a combat-tested Association fixer at his side. The Clan does not fight fair. They exploit the Night in the Backstreets and assassinate his wife right in front of him. All her Cinq training is worthless against an enemy that strikes from the shadows. Clyde is scarred (facial scarring possibly from the same attack). He withdraws from direct engagement entirely and shifts to a strategy of corporate growth from behind closed doors. The lesson: skill doesn't protect you, visibility kills you

### Years Before the Operation
- **Hector joins the Insurgence Clan:** As a young man in the outer Backstreets, Hector hits a wall - debt, poverty, or a threat he can't escape. The Insurgence Clan pulls him out when no one else will: augments, money, a place. He becomes an established operative within their network, eventually rising to run the Clan's unregistered augment shop in District 20. The Clan is the only structure that has ever offered him anything, and his debt to them grows deeper with every year

### ~2 Years Before Chapter 1
- **The Wells operation is assigned:** The Insurgence Clan's higher-ups identify Penny Wells as a target and discover that one of their own operatives - Hector - has a genuine childhood connection to her. They assign him the operation. His debt leaves no room to refuse. He spends the first year building a disposable cover identity (fake employment at a competitor prosthetics company, establishing return addresses for letters) and studying the Wells family's dynamics from a distance while the Clan's fixation on capturing Penny intensifies

### ~1 Year Before Chapter 1
- **The active operation begins:** Hector reconnects with Penny through letters, framing it as an old childhood friend reaching out. The letters are deliberately left noticeable enough for Clyde to intercept. Combat training begins. Clyde discovers the letters and starts surveilling Penny instead of confronting her - exactly as Hector predicted. Over the following year, Hector becomes Penny's closest confidant while Clyde watches in silence

### Chapter 1 - "Welcome to Prostheti Innovations"
- Players are hired as designers at Prostheti Innovations
- Meet Clyde Wells (guarded, professional, keeps everyone at arm's length)
- Meet Penny Wells (19, secretly training to become a Fixer)
- Meet Hector (Penny's childhood friend, training partner, employee at a competitor)
- Players work the design minigame, build trust with Penny, eventually train with her and Hector
- Background details emerge: Clyde's distant demeanor, hints of past tragedy, tension between father and daughter

### Chapter 2 - "The Test"
- Hector presents Penny with a "Fixer test": infiltrate a competitor's factory and destroy blueprints
- Players can recognize this as Syndicate work, not real Fixer work (no contract, no client, no pay)
- The factory is a trap - the director and augmented office workers ambush them
- Zwei Association Fixers break in, execute the director, extract everyone
- Clyde reveals he has been intercepting Penny's letters with Hector the entire time
- The Wells family fractures - Penny's trust in her father is shattered
- **Hidden:** Hector alerted Clyde the moment they entered the factory

### Chapter 3 - "Roadside Rush"
- Penny has gone missing - kidnapped during the Night in the Backstreets
- Clyde's Zwei are legally blocked from investigating (Night Taboo)
- Clyde tried to find Hector - his entire identity dissolved overnight (disposable cover)
- Clyde knows whoever took Penny had access to his systems and avoided every blind spot - sends the players (invisible, unregistered, won't be anticipated)
- Players confront two operatives at a refueling stop (Insurgence Clan, though players don't know this yet)
- One operative self-destructs via augments (first instance of control-through-hardware pattern)
- Investigation yields: message logs (unknown organization knows about Prostheti Innovations - Clan not named), records paper (unregistered augment shop, no TAA numbers), address paper (the shop's location)
- Message logs contain foreshadowing: complaint about "the warehouse manager letting another one go" (Julio)

### Chapter 4 - "Alleyway Investigation"
- Players infiltrate the Insurgence Clan's augment shop at the recovered address
- Four recruitable guards (Francis, Allen, Martha, Felix) - each joined the Clan because the City demanded they belong to something
- Francis mentions a warehouse run by an ex-Seven Fixer who lets captives go (Julio foreshadowing)
- The hidden boss is revealed as **Hector** - Penny's childhood friend, the "Major Controller"
- Boss fight with kill switch mechanic (second instance of control-through-hardware)
- Hector's interrogation reveals: long-time Clan operative (joined years earlier), assigned the Wells operation ~2 years ago because of his childhood connection to Penny, genuine friendship weaponized, two-year operation (one year cover-building, one year active infiltration), disposable cover identity, letters made noticeable to build mistrust between Penny and Clyde by engineering Clyde's secret surveillance, Nightwatcher's cloak for the kidnapping, Penny sent to a warehouse
- Hector's motivation: years of debt to the Clan left no room to refuse, belief that he was saving Penny from a Fixer death let him live with it
- Hector sent to Clyde. New lead: the warehouse

### Chapter 5 - "Hostage Negotiations"
- Players attempt to break into the Insurgence Clan warehouse - captured and restrained
- Interrogated by **Julio Smith** (Head of Negotiation, ex-Seven Fixer, Splitter faction)
- Julio's Seven training visible in his methodology. Truth Serum tea offered
- Two factions in the warehouse: Splitters (Julio's, pragmatic) vs. Steamworks (Simon's, militant)
- **Simon Jarvis** (Head of Security, Steamworks Agent) snaps - kills Julio over releasing hostages
- Simon's "conversion" language echoes the Tinkerer's ideology without fully understanding it
- Faction revolt gives players a chance to escape
- Two-floor dungeon: Simon's journal reveals "preparing the afflicted for transport to salvation," growing hatred from augment usage
- Simon boss fight with augment corruption glitch phase ([ERROR HA1R3D])
- Hostages from multiple districts found - but **Penny is not among them**
- Penny was transported to a ship. Warehouse notes confirm the Thumb allowed Clan operations on their territory

### Chapter 6 - "Oceanside Rescue"
- Penny traced to an Insurgence Clan transport ship on the Great Lake (far from U Corp. patrols)
- Players infiltrate via submarine - turn-based stealth through the ship
- Captain's red keycard obtained on the second floor
- **The Voice** speaks through the captain's computer - starts human, becomes increasingly inhuman. Refers to captives as "the afflicted," speaks of their emotions as a sickness to be cured. A red eye glimpsed through the screen. Sanity damage. Detonates a guard remotely
- Blood summons a **Carmine Whale** - the Clan deliberately using outside-City knowledge of the lake's ecosystem
- 12-round timed escape: free captives, fight Carmine Mermaids, reach submarines before the ship floods
- **Penny's augments activate in overdrive** - Insurgence Clan implants placed during captivity. The Tinkerer takes remote control from the Outskirts, forcing her to break out and escape in a locked submarine heading toward the Outskirts (third instance of control-through-hardware)
- Players do not rescue Penny. Something else moved her

### Chapter 7 - "Outskirts Venture"
- Clyde has tracked Penny to an Outskirts settlement. He is coming personally
- Clyde reveals his new weapon: Hector's confiscated augments, reverse-engineered. Causes him pain and voices with each shot
- The group travels via submarine through the Great Lake into the Outskirts
- The settlement is already burning - Insurgence Clan machines have invaded
- **Randal** (survivor) begs for help, thought Clyde sent the machines
- **Encounter 1:** First contact with red-eyed machine soldiers (Extractors, Harpooner). Machines retreat and carry away their dead. Clyde's weapon causes visible pain
- Between encounters: villagers turning on each other, shoving neighbors into machines' path, looting corpses. The worst of humanity on display
- **Encounter 2:** Engine-class machine with Extractor escorts. Clyde spots "Penny" running into a building and abandons the fight
- **Clyde falls:** Players find him with a hole through his chest. "Penny" morphs into the **Doll**. The Tinkerer descends
- **The Tinkerer's speech:** Systematic dismantling of the City's cruelty (the Night, cannibal districts, Singularities fueled by suffering, child labor, gambling mines, suicide vending machines, 80,000 unmourned dead, the Head's "ecology"). History of his people (the massacre, the Warlord's sacrifice, trapped by their migration). Why he targeted Penny specifically (agrees with Clyde's diagnosis, different prescription - save the person from their own humanity, not control the environment). The uncomfortable parallel between Clyde and the Tinkerer
- **The offer:** Nanites (prosthetic limb + permanent memory lock, fully transparent). Penny returned but emotionless. Accept = safety. Reject = hardest fight
- **Boss fight (if rejected):** The Doll + the Tinkerer. Doll builds Poise through evasion, unleashes Finishing Blow counters. "By your Command" at 0 HP
- **Clyde's last stand:** Rises from Insurgence tech, shoots the Tinkerer, forces retreat
- **Penny wakes:** With the Tinkerer's link severed, Penny partially regains awareness - fragmented but present. The conversion is not complete or not irreversible
- **Clyde lets go:** In his final moments, Clyde speaks to Penny directly for the first time. Admits he was wrong about how he protected her. Tells her she has the right to choose her own path. Asks *Penny* - not the players - to decide what happens to him. He is done choosing things for her
- **Penny's choice:** The campaign's emotional climax. Penny decides her father's fate: honor his request, refuse, or ask the players for help. The first real choice anyone has given her in the entire story
- **Departure:** Penny walks out of the Outskirts - unsteady, supported, damaged, but moving under her own power. The campaign ends. The story does not. What happens next is Penny's decision
